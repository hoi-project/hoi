for owners, mods & pinners

Running a community. Your keys, your call, your job.

A hoi community is a key, a handful of rules and the people who look after it. Nobody above you can overrule you, which is the point. It also means nobody above you can tidy up after you.

Who's responsible? You are.

The people who run a community are the people with control: its owners, its moderators and its pinners. They write the rules, decide who gets in, remove posts, ban keys and keep the history. So they're also the ones responsible for what happens there. In legal terms, the operators of a community may be its "provider".

The hoi project isn't. It wrote the software and that's all: it has no key in your community and no say in it. A public node someone runs, one of ours included, may hold a copy of an open community's public records, as any member's device does; it carries them and can stop carrying them, nothing more. It can't remove a post, ban someone or change anything, for you or for anyone asking about you. Private communities are encrypted to their members and reach no public node at all.

The short version

Your community, your rules, your ledger. Every action is signed and public to your members. Write rules people can follow, act on problems quickly, and say why.

who does what

Five roles, all held by members.

OOwners

The root keys named in the community's first record, with a threshold such as 2 of 4. Changing who moderates takes that many owner signatures on one ledger entry. New rules and limits take that many to propose, and the members decide. One lost laptop can't take the community over, and neither can its owners.

MModerators

Keys the owners add (and remove) in the ledger. They remove posts, ban and unban keys, label, pin and admit newcomers. Every action is a signed entry with a reason, visible to every member, and established members can veto it: removals wait 72 hours for that, and a moderator overturned three times is suspended.

PPinners

Members who keep the community's whole live window on their device or hoi-node, so newcomers can catch up. Moderators pin by default. Pinners hold what members post, so they share the responsibility for it, and they delete what the ledger removes.

EEstablished members

Members who have been vouched in and stayed a while. Reading a newcomer's posts without hiding them vouches for the newcomer; enough of them hiding a post makes it nap for everyone, and enough hiding someone's posts blocks them. They can also veto moderators. Quorums never exceed a third of them.

♥Members

Everyone who reads along. Newcomers post text; members add links; trusted members, whose posts the community responded to well, can share images and media. Their devices check every post against your rules and ledger, flag spam, and pick which labelers they trust on top of yours.

the ledger

Every action, signed and public.

The ledger is the community's append-only list of moderation actions. Each entry is signed by the key that took it, and copied to every member. Anyone holding the community's first record can check the whole thing, and any member can export it.

Remove

Every member's device deletes the post's body, keeps only its id and the reason, and refuses it if it shows up again. Readers see "Poof." with a link to the entry.

Ban & unban

A ban drops everything that key signs in this community from then on. An unban is just another entry. Automatic bans carry their evidence: the ids of the flags that triggered them, never the posts.

Rules, limits, mods

These need the owners' threshold of signatures in one entry, and they stay until replaced. Rules and limits are then proposals: everyone established when one was proposed decides. It applies once they all agree; people who are away (nothing of theirs left in the community) don't hold it up; after one fade window, silence counts as yes and only objections stop it. Everything else in the ledger fades with the community's posts.

limits

The dials, and where they start.

Every member's device enforces your limits before it shows anything. They live in the community's first record and change only when the owners propose it and the members agree. Start the way you mean to go on: changing them later is slow on purpose.

labelers

Labels, not deletes.

A labeler is any key that publishes labels: "spam", "off-topic", "needs a threat model", "illegal". Your community can name default labelers in its first record; each member adds their own or ignores yours. A labelled post isn't gone, it's napping, and anyone can peek.

Pick carefully

Default labelers shape what your members see first. Choose keys you know, and say why in your rules.

Your community's choices

The app ships with no default labelers. Your community chooses its rules and the labels it follows. Shared discovery and storage services do not gain a vote in those decisions.

"Illegal" is special

The app honours "illegal" labels from the labelers a member follows, and a removal for that reason takes effect at once, without the usual wait for vetoes.

what we can't do

No back door. Not even a side door.

So if someone asks the hoi project to act on your community, the honest answer is that we can't, and we'll point them to your moderators and your public ledger. That's not us dodging. It's the design.

before you invite people

A six-line checklist.

1Rules

Write rules a newcomer can read in a minute. Number them; ledger entries will cite them.

2Owners

At least two owners and a threshold. 2 of 3 is a good start.

3Limits

Set the stamp, the fade window, newcomer limits and admission. Start strict.

4Labelers

Choose ones you trust, or none.

5Pinners

Make sure someone keeps the history, ideally on an always-on hoi-node, or ask a public node to keep it available. The app shows how many copies it can see: a community is cold on one device, cool with one node holding it, warm with two, hot with three.

6Notices

When someone complains: read it, check it against your rules, act with a ledger entry, and give the reason. Everyone can see what you did, so say why.
