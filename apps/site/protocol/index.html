the protocol, in plain words

Signed notes, passed hand to hand. That's the whole trick.

Everything in hoi is a signed record. Communities are keys. Standing is earned from what the community itself signed. Sync is two devices comparing lists. Moderation is a public ledger the members can overturn. Deletion is a calendar. Here's how it fits together. The source and tests describe the current preview implementation; the wiki goes through all of it in detail.

1 · records

Everything is a record.

A post, a comment, a vote, a moderator's action: all one shape. The record is encoded as canonical DAG-CBOR, its id is the BLAKE3 hash of those bytes, and the author signs the id. Change one byte and the id changes, so the signature stops matching. parent says what a comment or vote is about, and expires_at can't be later than the community allows.

The envelopewire format v1
Record = {
  v:          1,
  kind:       "post" | "comment" | …,
  community:  <community key>,
  author:     <signing key>,
  parent:     <record id>?,
  created_at: <unix seconds>,
  expires_at: <unix seconds>,
  body:       { title, text, link?, name?, media? },
  nonce:      <stamp nonce>,
}
id  = BLAKE3(canonical bytes)
sig = Ed25519(author, id)

Kinds: post comment vote edit delete identity delegation revoke community ledger vouch review label report. Records never change: an edit is a new record that points at the old one. Size caps: 64 KiB for posts, 16 KiB for comments, 1 KiB for reviews, 512 bytes for votes, labels, vouches and identities. Anything that doesn't decode exactly is refused.

2 · keys

You are a key. Your devices are its helpers.

No email, no phone number, no password reset. Identity is a key pair on your device that did a few seconds of work, once. What you earn belongs to that key and can't be moved, sold or taken over.

Root key

An Ed25519 key, written as did:key, kept in an encrypted keystore (Argon2id + XChaCha20-Poly1305) or behind a passkey.

Identity work

An identity record proves Argon2id work over the key itself (10 bits by default). It can't be reused for another key, and each community says how much it asks for. Making a thousand keys costs a thousand times the work.

Device keys

Each device signs with its own key. The device signs the delegation (its consent) and the root blesses it, with a scope and an expiry (six months by default, never more than 400 days). A device belongs to the first root that blessed it, so nobody can claim your phone's posts. A signed revoke kicks a lost phone out.

No transfer

A new key starts from nothing in every community. A root can burn itself (revoking its own key), and then everything it signed is dropped everywhere. Losing a key costs its standing; that's the price of nobody being able to steal it.

Recovery

The root key split 3 of 5 (Shamir), each piece encrypted to a friend. Three friends bring you back; no server involved.

Names

Local nicknames by default, or a handle you prove with a DNS record (_hoi.example.org). The network key is separate from the posting key, so peers can't tie your address to your posts.

3 · stamps

About a second of work per post.

Before a record goes out, your device searches for a nonce so that BLAKE3(id ‖ nonce) starts with enough zero bits. Nothing for one post, a lot for a million. Every receiver re-checks it in microseconds.

4 · checks

Every device checks everything, in this order.

Whether a record arrives live, during catch-up or from your own keyboard, it passes the same stages before anything is shown, stored or passed on. The cheapest checks come first, so junk is turned away for the price of one hash. Fail one and it stops there.

Strikes

Each device keeps a fading score per key. Past the line, the key is blocked locally and the device may publish a signed report, with record ids as evidence and never the posts themselves.

Weighted reports

Reports count by the reporter's weight in your trust view (friends 1.0, friends of friends 0.5, long-time members 0.25, newcomers 0.05) or their standing in the community (trusted 1.0, established 0.5), whichever is larger. When the weighted sum reaches the community's limit, your device bans the key.

Peer scores

Peers that forward junk lose score and get disconnected. Spam relays end up talking to themselves.

5 · sync

Catching up is comparing lists.

New records spread live by gossip. Devices that were away catch up by comparing what they have with a peer, cheaply, and fetching only what's missing.

Live

A device passes each record it accepts, in a recs message, to every linked peer that shares the community, except the one it came from. Records it has seen before go no further. How sync works, message by message.

Catch-up

Range-based set reconciliation (Negentropy-style) over (created_at, id), limited to the community's live window. Two devices compare fingerprints of time ranges, split the ones that differ, then fetch the missing records by id over hoi/sync/1.

Who keeps what

Everyone keeps what they read. Pinners keep a community's whole live window and say so in gossip, so newcomers know whom to ask.

Ranking

"Hot" is computed on your device from votes weighted by your trust. Nobody ships tallies around, so two people can see different orders, and that's fine.

6 · ledger

Moderation is a public, signed list.

A community is an Ed25519 key pair. Its first record names the owners, a signature threshold and the limits. From then on, every moderation action is a ledger record with a sequence number, a reason and, for big changes, enough owner co-signatures. What that looks like.

Actions: rules limits mod-add mod-remove remove ban unban label pin unpin admit

remove

The record naps for 72 hours, so members can veto the removal. Then every device deletes its body, keeps its id and the reason ("Poof."), and refuses it if it comes back. Removals for illegal content or privacy take effect at once.

ban

Everything the key signs in this community is dropped from then on.

rules, limits, mods

Need co-signatures reaching the owners' threshold, and stay until replaced. Anyone with the first record can verify the whole list. The first record is the only one a community key ever gets to sign.

Changes by consent

New rules and limits are proposals. The people established when one is proposed answer with signed reviews: agree, or object with a reason. It applies once everyone agreed; while the only ones missing are away (nothing of theirs left), it's in effect; after one fade window, silence counts as yes and objections weighing half a voice stop it. People vouched in after the proposal don't decide it.

vetoes

Established members can veto removals, bans, unbans, labels, pins, admissions and new moderators. Enough vetoes overturn the entry on every device; a moderator overturned three times is suspended. Bans don't silence vetoers: the count ignores them.

7 · standing

Earn it, keep it by contributing.

What you may share in a community follows from records the community itself signed: who vouched for you, how long ago, how people responded to what you wrote. Every device computes the same answer from the same records. No moderator hands it out, and nobody can take it away alone.

RungHow you get thereWhat it adds
NewcomerA key with enough identity work. Marked "new here".Text, a few posts a day

EstablishedTwo weeks after you became a member (the community sets it)Your vouches, reviews and vetoes count
TrustedRecent posts and comments the community responded to wellImages, audio and video. Stop contributing and it fades.
Reading is vouching

When an established member has read a couple of a newcomer's posts and didn't hide them, their app vouches for the newcomer. Validation happens by people simply reading. Objections are signed too, and need a reason.

Hiding

Established members can hide a post for spam, abuse, illegal content, being off-topic or breaking a numbered rule. Disagreeing isn't a reason. A post naps for everyone when enough of them hide it and they outnumber those who said "looks good" two to one. Hidden posts stay peekable.

Blocking

Someone whose posts enough members hid is blocked from the community: nothing they write is passed on. It lifts by itself as the reasons expire or others speak up for them.

Voices, not heads

Hides, blocks, vetoes and objections weigh voices. Each established member gives away at most one voice a quarter, split among everyone they vouched for, and nobody holds more than their strongest voucher. Inviting all your friends at once brings them in, but not as a voting bloc.

Keeping it fair

Quorums never exceed a third of the established voice, and every decision needs two people. People who hide posts the community defends lose their say; people who vouch in spammers lose theirs. Fresh keys count for nothing, so an army of them can't hide or block anyone. Vouches that turn up claiming to be older than a device's last sync count from when they turned up.

Media

PNG, JPEG, GIF, WebP, Ogg Opus, MP4 and WebM, nothing scriptable. The sender's app strips location, device names and every other hidden part; every receiver checks the file is exactly what it claims and nothing more. Devices fetch media only from trusted members.

8 · expiry

Time does the deleting.

Every record carries expires_at, no later than the community's maximum retention (180 days by default). Past it, every compliant device deletes the record and leaves it out of reconciliation, so it can't sneak back in.

Saved posts

You can keep a post you saved, on your device only. It's never shared again after it expires.

What stays

The community's first record, the current rules, limits and moderator list until replaced, and the keys, identities and vouches standing is built from.

The honest limit

A modified app could ignore expiry and keep things. Expiry makes forgetting the default everywhere; it can't force a stranger's disk.

9 · discovery

Finding each other with zero project servers.

There's no bootstrap server, no directory and no default relay list. The app ships with an empty relay list and an empty labeler list; the defaults arrive with the invite that brought you in.

An invite linkall you need to join
hoi://join?code=<checksummed, masked CBOR:
  community key, relays, peers,
  the community's genesis>
Invite links & QR

Carry the community key plus a few relays and peers to start from, masked so no address is readable in the link itself. Every link format.

Local network

Native devices send a UDP multicast beacon (239.255.70.74:7848) every 30 seconds for each public community they joined, sealed so only its members can read where to connect.

Mainline DHT

Public nodes run the BitTorrent DHT among themselves, with no outside bootstrap, and announce the open communities they carry. A lookup gets signed, short-lived routes.

Node lists

Public nodes pass on the public nodes they reached themselves, never ones they were only told about, with the uptime they saw. Devices keep up to 32 and forget those that stopped answering for 30 days. How discovery works.

10 · relays

Relays are dumb on purpose.

A packet relay is a plain iroh relay, which hoi-node --packet-relay runs beside a node. It forwards encrypted QUIC packets by destination node id from an in-memory table, and that's it. The protocol assumes relays are untrusted: they see node ids, timing and sizes, never what's inside.

Members only

With --packet-relay-members, a node's relay serves only endpoints it knows as members of the communities it carries; its operator can also block endpoints. Packet relay.

Browsers

Browsers can't accept connections, so they reach nodes over WebRTC and never through a packet relay. The app says which node it is talking to. Transports.

Run one

Keeping communities reachable is a switch in the desktop app; a packet relay is a flag on hoi-node. What the switch does and what you agree to.

11 · versions

Growing up without breaking.

Every record says which protocol version it speaks (v), and devices announce what they support when they connect. Records from an unknown version are refused, and an app more than one major version behind what it sees on the network asks to be updated.

12 · where your data lives

What runs where, and what it handles.

This website

No accounts, collection forms, analytics, tracking cookies or fingerprinting. The examples and animations run locally in your browser; demo text is not submitted anywhere. Fonts, scripts and images are served with the site. Visiting it does not connect you to the community network.

The browser app

The app is a frontend: its JavaScript, WebAssembly and application logic run in your browser on your machine. The files are static and name no node, registry or relay; whatever serves them does not run the community application, receive your posts, or act as a registry, public node or private relay. Your browser contacts directly the nodes it learns of: its home node, the routes in invites and node links you open, and the public nodes those pass on. Nobody can reach a browser, so what you post from one is readable by others only once a home node carrying the community has it.

On your device

Your device stores your identity, private keys, settings and community data. The browser vault encrypts saved identity and community data using your local passphrase. The app also caches its static files and remembers verified public name/key bindings locally. Clearing browser storage can remove your keys, saved data and name bindings; losing the passphrase can prevent recovery.

Registry nodes

Optional, and not used by the published app. Anyone can run a registry, as a role of the node program (hoi-node --registry) or as a separate service; a registry can mirror another registry’s signed listings, and when none is reachable two communities may carry the same name under different keys. A registry serves public name-to-community-key mappings, signed proofs, protocol versions and public peer routes. It has no website, account system, posts, attachments, profiles or private membership lists. Name reservations persist; node announcements stop being listed after 30 minutes and expired announcements are pruned when new announcements arrive. Each registry has its own namespace; a name is not a global identity.

Public nodes

Every home node is a public node unless its person turns that off, and anyone can run one with no key of its own (hoi-node --public). It tells whoever connects about the other public nodes it reached itself, never about what it was told. It accepts peer connections and stores and serves signed public community records, including posts and media, according to the communities it carries and their retention rules. It carries open communities that connected members hand it, and those a registry lists if it is given one (the community’s signed definition only, never routes or anything about the member); it keeps nothing about who asked, lets go of a community nobody else held for two weeks or whose records never arrived, and deletes what it let go. Whoever runs it can refuse a community for good. It has no person’s key and signs nothing. Public content is readable by anyone, including the nodes carrying it.

Private communities

Invited devices encrypt private messages and attachments before sending them to an encrypted relay. The relay stores padded ciphertext and delivery metadata: a room key that changes whenever membership changes, rounded sizes, signatures, upload and read times, and expiry times. It does not receive member keys or community identifiers. App defaults expire these objects within 30 days; the relay protocol permits at most 31 days. Invited members can read and retain their own copies. Removing a member changes future keys, not copies already received.

Connections

Every server you connect to, whether it serves files or is a registry, public node or relay, sees your IP address and when you connect. The hoi software adds no access logs of its own.

What you share

Public posts, profile details and signed actions are shared with the public communities you use. Reports are signed community records, not confidential messages to moderators. Don’t put private contact information in public names, posts or reports. The client checks signatures locally; signatures establish control of a key, not a person’s identity.

Retention and removal

Participating devices apply community expiry and removal rules. Peers, recipients, exports or backups can retain copies. Turning off a node does not erase its database.
