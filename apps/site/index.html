Small communities around shared interests, living on their members' devices. Public conversations are open to everyone. Private conversations are encrypted for invited members, with shared storage keeping them available between visits. Your community sets its rules. hoi is an informal hi; the .sh is you keeping your voice down.

1 · a post is a signed note

Your device signs it. Everyone's device checks it.

Every device checks the records it receives. A post is a tiny note your key signs, stamped with about a second of work, then handed to the people subscribed to the community. Each of them verifies it before showing it. Try it.

2 · spam bounces off

Nobody is on spam duty. Everybody is.

Every device checks the signature, the stamp and the rate limit before a post is even shown. Flood the network and the network drops you, automatically and with signed evidence from the people who saw it. These checks limit automated abuse; members and moderators still handle harmful content that passes them.

3 · communities set retention

Posts have a lifetime.

Each community sets how long posts live. Participating devices apply that expiry window and signed moderation decisions. Members can choose which labels they trust. Recipients, exports and backups can retain copies, so expiry is not a promise that every copy disappears.

who runs what

Static website. Local app. Independent nodes.

This page is informational: its demos run locally and submit nothing. The browser app runs its code on your machine. The static host serves application files, with no node, registry or relay named in them; your client finds communities through the nodes people share with you and connects directly. To post, get the home node. If you choose to make it a public node, one you keep running is a place others find communities; otherwise it publishes no address of its own.
