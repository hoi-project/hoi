Communities & the ledger

A community is a key pair, one signed first record, and a signed list of moderation actions. This page covers the community record and every limit in it, the ledger and each of its actions, co-signatures, how a device replays the ledger into community state, removals and their veto window, and how rules and limits change only when the community agrees.

The community record

Every community starts with one record of kind community, often called the genesis. Its body names the community, its owners, how many owners must sign big changes, and the limits every device enforces. The record is signed by the community's own key: author and community are the same 32 bytes, and the shape check refuses anything else (genesis must be signed by the community key).

A genesis never changes. A device that already holds one for a key refuses a second (a community's genesis never changes), so the first record a device sees for a key is the one it keeps. Changes after that go through the ledger.

The envelope

A genesis has no parent. Its expires_at is created_at plus KEEP, 100 years (100 * 365 * 86_400 seconds), so it outlives every retention window. It is stamped to GLOBAL_POW_BITS, 16 bits, because it belongs to no community whose difficulty could apply. Its wire size cap is 64 KiB, like posts and ledger entries. See records and stamps.

The community key

create_community generates a fresh Ed25519 key pair for the community. That key signs exactly three things: the genesis, and, when the name has a canonical public form, one name-claim statement and one publication statement (below). Then the device forgets the secret. The code says why:

let published = self.publish(r);
// The community key signs its genesis and, for registry-compatible names, one fixed
// name-claim statement. The owners govern through the ledger. Forgetting the key means
// nobody, not even a thief of this device, can sign a rival genesis or a different claim.
self.community_kps.remove(&c);

So the community key is an address and a seal on the first record, nothing more. Nobody holds it afterwards. Governance comes from the owner keys named in the genesis.

What creating a community does

  1. Limits from the draft

    The draft's limits patch is applied on top of Limits::default() and checked with hoi_validate::limits. For an open (non-private) community whose draft says nothing about it, scatter_open is set to true, so posts travel in pieces (scattered delivery).

  2. Owners and threshold

    The creator's root key comes first, then any co-owner keys from the draft, each once. The threshold defaults to 1 and must be between 1 and the number of owners.

  3. Sign and forget

    The genesis is made, its content checked, the registration and publication statements signed if the name allows, the genesis published, and the community key dropped.

  4. Joined and pinned

    The creator's device joins with pinning on, so it keeps the whole live window.

  5. First rules

    If the draft has rules and the threshold is 1, they go in as a rules entry right away. With a higher threshold the draft rules are dropped; the owners set them together from the Manage tab.

The validator explains the quorum floor: quorums below two would let one person hide, block or overturn alone. The ladder also caps every quorum at a third of the established voice, so a quorum set high stays reachable (quorums).

One encoding per value

scatter, trackers and age_issuers are written only when they differ from the default. Communities on the default encode exactly as they did before those fields existed, so older decoders, which refuse unknown fields, still read them. The decoder refuses the default written out (the default scatter setting is left out) and an empty trackers or age_issuers list: one value, one encoding, stable ids and signatures. See records.

Which limits a record answers to

When limits change, records already made don't become invalid. limits_for takes, for four fields, the more lenient of the limits in effect when the record says it was made and the limits in effect now:

pub(crate) fn limits_for(c: &Community, created_at: u64) -> Limits {
    let then = c.limits_at(created_at);
    let mut l = c.limits.clone();
    l.pow_bits = l.pow_bits.min(then.pow_bits);
    l.max_retention = l.max_retention.max(then.max_retention);
    l.newcomer_posts_per_day = l.newcomer_posts_per_day.max(then.newcomer_posts_per_day);
    l.ladder.identity_bits = l.ladder.identity_bits.min(then.ladder.identity_bits);
    l
}

Members took a post under the limits of its day, so a device that joins after the community tightened them takes it too. A record made since needs today's stamp and today's window: a test shows a device that joined late refusing a post stamped to the old difficulty and one that expires later than today's retention allows. Community::limits_at(t) answers from limits_since, the list of every limits change and when it took effect.

Open, approve, invite, age proof, private

Two separate questions decide what kind of place a community is. limits.admission decides who may post and whose posts are shown. Whether the community is private decides who can read at all. Joining rules alone never hide posts from readers: an open, approve or invite community's records are ordinary signed records, readable by every device that carries the community.

Private communities

A private community is one this device marks private (state.private_communities) or holds an MLS group for. Its records travel encrypted to members through encrypted relays and never through plain sync (private communities). A private group whose admission is invite or approve "follows its records": the owner's device adds the people the ledger and ladder admit and removes the people the ledger bans, and every member refuses a membership commit the records don't back. The owner of a public invite or approve community can make a private copy of it (private_copy): a new community with the same name and limits, kept as a private group.

Where admission matters elsewhere

  • Only open, non-private communities can be published in a public directory: community_publication refuses anything else (Only open communities can be published in the public directory.).
  • A node announcing itself to a registry lists only communities whose current limits and genesis both say open: at most 64, keeping the most recently joined when there are more.
  • Labels, trust statements, blocklists and tallies are for public communities; the app refuses to publish them in private ones.
  • The web app's New Community form offers open and approve, with invite and age-proof shown disabled; the Manage tab's limits form offers all four.

The ledger

Every moderation action is a record of kind ledger in the community. The ledger is not a separate file or log: it is all the ledger records a device holds for the community. Any device holding the genesis can verify all of it, and replaying it from the genesis gives the same state on every device (replay).

The entry's body

sequ64
A sequence number. The app writes the highest seq it has replayed plus one (below).
actiontext
One of the 13 actions in the table below.
targetbytes32, optional
A record id (remove, label, pin, unpin) or a person's root key (ban, unban, admit, mod-add, mod-remove, retire). Required for those ten actions.
reasontext, optional
At most 500 characters. For label it is the label itself and must not be empty. The app asks for one on every removal (Say why. The reason goes in the public ledger.).
evidence[bytes32], optional
At most 256 record ids. An automatic ban carries the ids of the reports behind it, never the posts.
cosign[{key, sig}], optional
At most 16 co-signatures over the entry's proposal id (co-signatures).

The full rules text, required on rules. Checked as markdown of at most 20,000 characters and 1,000 lines.

The full new limits, required on limits and checked like a genesis's.
drop_keymap, optional
Only on drop-key, and required there: a key and its secret sealed once to each of at most 64 devices (scattered delivery). A drop-key entry has no target.
patchbytes, optional
Only on rules, and required there: the change as a Loro update of 1 byte to 64 KiB (rules as a document).

A ledger record has no parent and must name a community. Its wire cap is 64 KiB. Its stamp is the community's pow_bits minus 8, like votes and reviews. A device key signs it under a delegation scope of * or moderate:<community hex> (devices). At ingest the node refuses ledger records from anyone who isn't an owner or moderator now, or, for threshold actions, an owner (only owners and moderators write the ledger).

pub fn needs_threshold(&self) -> bool {
    matches!(self, LedgerAction::Rules | LedgerAction::Limits | LedgerAction::ModAdd | LedgerAction::ModRemove | LedgerAction::Retire)
}

/// Kept until superseded instead of expiring with the retention window.
pub fn kept_until_superseded(&self) -> bool {
    self.needs_threshold() || *self == LedgerAction::DropKey
}

pub fn vetoable(&self) -> bool {
    matches!(
        self,
        LedgerAction::Remove
            | LedgerAction::Ban
            | LedgerAction::Unban
            | LedgerAction::Label
            | LedgerAction::Pin
            | LedgerAction::Admit
            | LedgerAction::ModAdd
            | LedgerAction::Retire
    )
}

"Kept" means the entry carries an expiry 100 years out (KEEP) and sits outside the retention window; nothing deletes it when a later entry replaces it, the replay simply applies the later one. Every other entry expires with the community's max_retention like a post, and when it expires its effect goes with it: a ban entry that fades stops banning. See what is exempt from expiry.

Sequence numbers

ledger_record gives each new entry seq: st.max_seq + 1, where max_seq is the highest seq the replay has seen. Nothing requires the numbers to be gapless or unique: two moderators acting at once can write the same number. The replay orders entries by (at, seq, id), so seq orders entries that share an effective time, and the id breaks any remaining tie the same way on every device.

Effective time

An entry's at is its effective time: its created_at, or the time this device first saw it, if it arrived from elsewhere claiming a time more than an hour (LATE_GRACE) before this device last finished catching up with the community. It wasn't out there when this device looked, so it counts from when it turned up, and a backdated entry can't rewrite what already happened. The same rule covers vouches and reviews (late records).

Co-signatures and the owners' threshold

With a threshold above 1, a threshold action needs that many distinct owner roots behind one entry. Owners can't each sign the final record, because its id depends on the author, the nonce and the co-signatures themselves. So co-signers sign something that stays fixed: the entry's proposal_id.

v, kind, community, parent | as published | sky | 4 | unchanged created_at, expires_at | as published | sky | 3 | unchanged author | 32 B | coral | 3 | all zeros nonce | 0 | coral | 2 | the stamp is mined later body: seq, action, target, reason, text, limits, patch… | as published | mint | 8 | unchanged body.cosign | — | peach | 4 | left out 
pub fn proposal_id(&self) -> Id {
    let mut r = self.clone();
    if let Body::Ledger(l) = &mut r.body {
        l.cosign = None;
    }
    r.nonce = 0;
    r.author = Id::ZERO;
    r.id()
}

Whichever owner collects the last signature publishes the entry under their own device key and signs its full id as usual. The conformance vector vectors/threshold.json pins a rules entry with threshold 2 of 3 owners, published by one owner and co-signed by another, with its proposal_id.

Collecting signatures

lanes: Owner A | Owner B | Every device -- Propose -- Owner A: builds the entry, signs its proposal_id Owner A -> Owner B: hoi:cosign: code (base64url of the record), have 1, need 2 -- Co-sign -- Owner B: checks it is an owner, the action needs a threshold, the record is under 7 days old Owner B: keeps only co-signatures that verify, adds its own Owner B => Every device @mint: publishes under its own device key, freshly stamped -- Replay -- Every device: counts distinct owner roots behind valid co-signatures, as of created_at

Each co-signature is {key, sig}: the signing key and an Ed25519 signature over the 32-byte proposal id. The app co-signs with the root key if this device holds it, otherwise with the device key. In the replay, owner_signers counts the publisher's root if it is an owner, then every co-signature that verifies and whose key maps to an owner root as of the entry's creation time: a device whose blessing had expired or been revoked by then counts for nothing, so a stolen and revoked laptop can't co-sign owner decisions. The publisher must itself be an owner (only owners sign this), and fewer distinct owners than the threshold leaves the entry refused (not enough owner signatures).

A threshold entry carrying co-signatures doesn't have to be fresh on arrival the way live gossip must be (within ten minutes), because collecting signatures takes time. But one published from this device, or arriving live, more than 7 days (PROPOSAL_MAX_AGE) after it was created is refused as too old, and cosign refuses codes older than a week (That proposal is more than a week old. Start a new one.).

Replaying the ledger

A community's state is a pure function of its genesis, the ledger entries a device holds and the community's own judgements. hoi_moderation::Community holds that state, and the node rebuilds it from scratch every time, so arrival order never matters.

Order and checks

// entries at their effective time; accepted proposals take effect when they were decided
// (a decision sorts right after its proposal's entry when both fall on the same moment)
let mut steps: Vec<(u64, u64, Id, u8, Step)> =
    entries.iter().map(|e| (e.at, e.body.seq, e.id, 0, Step::Entry(e))).collect();
steps.extend(pen.passed.iter().map(|(id, t)| (*t, seq_of.get(id).copied().unwrap_or(0), *id, 1, Step::Decided(*id))));
steps.sort_by_key(|a| (a.0, a.1, a.2, a.3));

Each entry is checked against the state just before it:

  1. Overturned?

    If the community vetoed it, it is refused: overturned by the community.

  2. Suspended signer?

    If its signer is a moderator the community suspended and the entry is from on or after the suspension, it is refused: the moderator was suspended by the community. Owners are never suspended.

  3. Threshold actions

    The signer must be an owner and the distinct owner signers must reach the threshold. rules and limits become proposals (a limits entry without limits is refused: no limits); mod-add, mod-remove and retire take effect.

  4. drop-key

    Any one owner, carrying a drop key.

  5. Everything else

    The signer must be an owner or moderator at that moment (only owners and moderators sign this) and the entry must have a target (no target).

After the replay, any moderator the community suspended who was added on or before the suspension is taken out of mods. An entry refused now can take effect later: if an earlier entry shows up (say, the mod-add that made its signer a moderator), the next replay applies both in order. The threshold_and_mods test feeds a moderator's removal before the mod-add that empowers it and gets the removal applied.

Replaying with the ladder

The ladder decides which entries the community overturned, which moderators it suspended and which proposals it accepted, and those feed back into the replay. Community::settle runs ladder::settle for up to 6 rounds, replaying the ledger whenever a round changes the overturned entries, the suspensions or the passed proposals, until nothing changes, then replays once more with the final penalties so the ledger state matches the standing it keeps. A node settles a community whenever its records changed, and otherwise whenever it settles and a minute has passed since the last time, so removals ripen and people become established on time. The details are on standing.

Removals and the contest window

A remove entry doesn't delete anything at once. The replay writes a tombstone whose effective_at is the entry's time plus the community's contest_window (72 hours by default, at most 14 days). Until then the record naps: it stays stored and readable, the feed marks it removal pending: <reason> by the moderator's name, and established members can veto the removal.

/// Removals that can't wait for the contest window.
pub fn removal_is_urgent(reason: &str) -> bool {
    let r = reason.trim().to_ascii_lowercase();
    r.starts_with("illegal") || r.starts_with("privacy")
}

A reason that starts with illegal or privacy, in any letter case, takes effect at once: effective_at is the entry's own time. For illegal removals the node also deletes the post's media and refuses those bytes, and pictures that look like them, from then on, before it deletes the body (once the body is gone there's nothing left to blocklist).

When the window ends

At each sweep the node marks a community dirty when one of its removals has ripened, and the rebuild skeletonizes every record whose tombstone is effective: the body is removed from the store and from disk, and a Skeleton keeps the community, parent, author, kind, times, who removed it, why and which entry. Replies under it stay, under a "Poof." box. The id goes into the store's deleted set until the record's own expires_at, so reconciliation never asks for it again, and if the record arrives anyway, ingest refuses it as removed. See tombstones.

If the community overturns a removal after it took effect, the rebuild finds a skeleton whose removal entry no longer stands, drops the skeleton and forgets the id from the deleted set. The record isn't conjured back: it returns with the next sync from a device that still has it.

The vector

vectors/tombstone.json holds a comment and an owner's remove entry for it with the reason "rule 4, undisclosed vendor". After the removal, compliant nodes delete the comment's body, keep its id and the entry, and refuse to ingest it again.

Bans and unbans

A ban targets a person's root key. From the ban's time on, every record that person signs in the community with a created_at at or after the ban is refused at ingest (author is banned here). What they posted before stays, subject to the usual expiry, hides and removals. A ban also removes a moderator role the target held. Owners can't be banned, in the replay (owners can't be banned) or in the app; nor can a person ban themselves from the app.

An unban is just another entry. Both are vetoable. Because ban and unban entries expire with the retention window, a ban lasts as long as its entry: after max_retention it fades unless someone bans again.

  • Bans don't silence vetoers. The ladder works out who may veto, and who decides proposals, as if ledger bans didn't exist, so a moderator can't ban the people who would overturn the ban (vetoes).
  • Automatic bans. When reports weighted by trust reach autoban_threshold, a device bounces the key locally; on an owner's or moderator's device the ban also goes into the ledger, with the report ids as evidence (autobans).
  • On this device only. A key bounced by this device alone, not banned in the ledger, is let back in by unban without signing anything.
  • Private groups. In a private group that follows its records, a ban is what makes the owner's device remove the member; a removal without a ban is refused (private communities).

Labels, pins and admissions

Labels

A label entry puts its label text in reason. The replay keeps one label of each text per record: a newer entry with the same label replaces the older. Labels that hide are a fixed list, compared case-insensitively:

/// Labels that make a record "napping" (hidden, peekable). Anything else is shown as a note.
pub const HIDING_LABELS: [&str; 6] = ["spam", "likely spam", "hide", "nsfw", "illegal", "off-topic"];

A hiding label from the ledger makes the record nap, attributed to "the mods"; any other label shows as a note next to it. Ledger labels are separate from labels published by labelers, which count only where someone follows the labeler (labelers).

Pins

A pin adds the target to pins; unpin and any removal of the target take it out. When a feed is asked for one community, pinned posts come first, then the chosen order. Pins are vetoable; unpins aren't.

Admissions

An admit entry adds the target to admitted and becomes an admission the ladder reads. If the signer was an owner or moderator at that moment, the admission also counts as their vouch when voice is shared out (voice). Admits are how approve communities let people in from the Manage tab, and the only way into an age-proof community, where the entry's reason is exactly age proof. Admits are vetoable.

Changes by consent

New rules and new limits don't apply when the owners sign them. A rules or limits entry that reaches the threshold becomes a Proposal, and the people who were established when it was proposed decide. The ladder's decide function works out where each proposal stands.

pub struct Proposal {
    pub id: Id,
    pub action: LedgerAction,
    pub at: u64,
    pub signers: Vec<Id>,
    pub base: Limits,           // the limits in effect when it was made
    pub limits: Option<Limits>,
    pub text: Option<String>,
    pub patch: Option<Vec<u8>>,
    pub deadline: u64,          // at + max_retention
    pub applied_at: Option<u64>,
}

A limits proposal is a patch

A limits entry carries a full set of limits, but it changes only the fields in which it differs from base, the limits in effect when it was made. patch_limits(base, want, cur) applies just those fields to whatever is in effect when it passes, so two proposals about different things, decided in either order, don't undo each other. Proposal::changes() lists the changed fields as (field, from, to), which is what the Rules tab shows. The fields it compares are every limit and ladder setting, scatter_resist, scatter_open, trackers and age_issuers.

Who decides

The electors are the people established when the proposal was made, worked out with the ladder settings in effect then (its admit_vouches and established_age). Ledger bans are ignored for this, so a moderator can't ban the people who would object; burned identities, people the community blocked and struck vouchers are left out, except owners. People vouched in after the proposal don't decide it, however many there are: a test vouches in ten friends right after a proposal, has them object once established, and the proposal still passes. Each elector weighs their voice at the moment of the proposal (voice).

Answers

An elector answers with a review whose parent is the proposal's entry: ok to agree, veto with a reason (up to 200 characters) to object, none to take an answer back. The owners who signed the entry agree by signing; the app refuses an answer from them (You proposed it: your signature already says yes.). Every answer to a proposal is kept as long as the proposal, because when people agreed decides it; only the earliest 16 per person count (MAX_ANSWERS), and nodes refuse the rest. Answers made at or after the deadline don't count, and nodes refuse them (answers to that proposal closed). An established member's device shows a notice when a proposal arrives: "They apply only if everyone established agrees. Have your say on the Rules tab."

Where a proposal stands

Voices are integers: one voice is VOICE = 720,720. DISSENT, half a voice (360,360), is what it takes to stop a change.

StatusWhenEffect
passed, unanimousAt the first moment the electors who hadn't agreed weighed less than half a voice. Retired electors who never answered don't count against it.In effect from that moment, for good. Later answers don't undo it.
in-effectBefore the deadline, while the electors who object, plus those who are around (something of theirs is live and not hidden or removed) and haven't answered, weigh less than half a voice.Applied for as long as that holds. If someone who was away comes back and objects, it stops again.

The deadline is one retention window after the proposal because by then everything anyone had posted before it has faded: everyone who was around had their chance. While nobody but the proposing owner is established, the proposer's own signature is unanimous, and the change applies at once; that's what the Manage tab means by "While nobody else is established, it applies at once."

The ladder vector

vectors/ladder.json pins a proposal with six electors: the owner agrees, one member agrees, one objects with a quarter of a voice, three are away. Its expected status is in-effect, with agreed_voice 900,900 and objecting_voice 180,180.

Retiring an elector

Someone who left for good would hold up every change until its deadline. Owners can sign a retire entry (a threshold action) for an elector who has signed nothing in the community, and been a member, for at least 30 days before the entry (RETIRE_AWAY). It takes effect 7 days later (RETIRE_NOTICE), so the person can come back: anything they sign voids it. From then on their silence holds nothing up. It never takes away an answer they gave: someone who objected and then left keeps their objection. Owners can't be retired, and the app refuses to retire anyone who signed something or joined in the last 30 days. Retirements are vetoable.

Taking effect

A proposal that passed, or is in effect, enters the replay as a decision at its time, sorted right after the proposal's own entry when both fall on the same second. A limits decision patches the current limits and appends them to limits_since. A rules decision makes the rules version one higher (the first is version 1) with that proposal's signers. The app lists proposals on the Rules tab until 30 days after they passed or lapsed.

Rules and the wiki as documents

Rules text and wiki pages are Loro CRDT documents, so changes written against the same version merge instead of overwriting each other.

Rules

A rules entry carries the full text its proposers saw (text) and the change itself as a Loro update to the rules document as it stood when they wrote it (patch, at most 64 KiB). rules_patch builds it: it rebuilds the document from the passed proposals, sets a fresh random Loro peer id (never 0 or 1), replaces the text, commits, and exports the updates since the old version. The rules shown are the passed entries' patches imported in the order they passed:

pub(crate) fn rules_doc(&self, c: &Id) -> Option<LoroDoc> {
    let st = self.communities.get(c)?;
    let mut passed: Vec<_> = st.proposals.values().filter(|p| p.action == LedgerAction::Rules && p.applied_at.is_some() && p.patch.is_some()).collect();
    passed.sort_by_key(|p| (p.applied_at, p.id));
    let doc = LoroDoc::new();
    for p in passed {
        let _ = doc.import(p.patch.as_deref().unwrap_or_default());
    }
    Some(doc)
}

The two_rules_changes_written_against_the_same_version_both_count test proposes two changes to "1. Be kind." from the same version; once both pass, the rules read both changes merged, at version 3.

The wiki

A community's wiki is a set of pages, each a Loro document built from records of kind wiki, each carrying one update. Edits merge whatever order they arrive in; an edit whose predecessors haven't arrived waits inside the document.

Registration and publication proofs

A public registry maps names to community keys (registry). Only the community key can claim a name for itself, and it is forgotten at creation, so the creating device signs both statements up front and keeps them as proofs in its local state (registration_proofs, publication_proofs).

The canonical name

community_registration_name trims the genesis name and lowercases it. It is valid if it is 1–32 bytes, starts with a-z or 0-9, and uses only a-z, 0-9, ., _ and -. A name that doesn't qualify gets no proofs; the community still works and can be shared by invite.

The statements

pub fn community_registration_statement(name: &str, community: &Id) -> String {
    format!("hoi community name v1\n{name}\n{}", community.hex())
}

pub fn community_publication_statement(name: &str, community: &Id, protocol: &str, index: u32) -> String {
    format!("hoi community index v2\n{name}\n{}\n{protocol}\n{index}", community.hex())
}

The community key signs the exact UTF-8 bytes with Ed25519. The publication statement also binds the protocol family and index edition this build was compiled with, read from release.json (protocolVersion and indexVersion), so runtime notice edits can't relabel a signed community. The vectors pin both with a fixed seed:

  "community": "197f6b23e16c8532c6abc838facd5ea789be0c76b2920334039bfa8b3d368d61",
  "protocolVersion": "0.3",
  "indexVersion": 1,
  "statement": "hoi community index v2\ngarden_1.v2-test\n197f6b23…8d61\n0.3\n1",

}

community-registration.json holds the same name and key with the v1 name statement. Both vectors are read by the hoi-core discovery tests and the registry's protocol tests.

When proofs are handed out and restored

  • community_registration refuses private communities and names without a canonical form, and needs the stored proof (Register from the device that created it).
  • community_publication also needs admission to be open.
  • A key file carries the proofs of public communities this device holds, at most 64 of each kind (MAX_BACKUP_PROOFS). On restore, a proof is kept only if the community is joined, not private, its genesis name canonicalizes to the proof's name, and the community key's signature over the statement verifies.
