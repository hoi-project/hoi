Spam & rate limits

There is no spam filter in the middle, because there is no middle. Every device is the bouncer: it counts each key's records against rolling windows, spots the same words pasted again and again, keeps a fading score of strikes per key, bounces keys past the line, signs reports with record ids as evidence, and drops peers that forward junk. This page covers every window, constant and formula, and what the simulator's attacks show.

Where spam meets the pipeline

Every record passes the same checks, in order, whether it arrives live, during catch-up or from this device's own keyboard (validation pipeline). The cheap checks come first, so junk costs the receiver as little as possible. The spam defences sit at these points in Node::ingest:

  1. Stamp floor, before any signature

    The least work any key could need in this community for this kind, checked with one hash. A flood of unstamped records costs a hash each, not a signature check (stamps).

  2. Signature, identity work, the full stamp, time

    Bad bytes and bad signatures cost the forwarding peer score (peer scores). A key needs memory-hard identity work, so making a thousand keys costs a thousand times the work (identity).

  3. Bans, removals, bounces

    A banned key's new records and removed records are refused. A key this device bounced has its records refused and counted (bounced_records).

  4. Rate

    The key's records of this kind in this community, counted by created_at against rolling windows (rate windows). Breaking one on live gossip is a strike.

  5. Content, then copy-paste floods

    Text, link and media rules (content rules), then the same words again (floods). Both are strikes when a peer sent the record.

  6. Standing

    What the author may share here. Beyond it, records are held, not refused (holds).

Rate windows

hoi_validate::rate_windows says how many records of a kind one author may have in a community within each window. A record must fit every window listed for it. Newcomer here means the ladder hasn't admitted the author yet; owners and moderators have no windows at all.

An hour is 3,600 seconds, a day 86,400. A window with a budget of 0 allows nothing: a community that sets newcomer_posts_per_day to 0 lets newcomers comment but not post. The day budget for newcomer posts is the more lenient of the limits in effect when the record says it was made and today's (which limits apply).

Rate groups

A window counts the author's records in the same rate group, so edits and deletes share one budget, and so do vouches and reviews, and labels and reports:

Posts with and without media are one group with different budgets: a member's media post must fit 4 an hour and 6 a day counting all their posts.

A key posts in bursts. Each post is placed on a timeline at its created_at, and every one-hour and one-day span around it is counted. Posts that would put any span over its budget are dropped; the rest are accepted, whenever they claim to have been written.

Windows roll: they count by each record's own time, not by when it arrived.

Counting by created_at

Windows count records by the time they say they were made, not by when they arrived. That makes the limit the same on every device, and closes the gap where backdated records, arriving as catch-up, would skip the limit. The store keeps, per (community, author root), every record's (created_at, id), and author_times returns the times of those in the record's rate group.

/// Whether a record at `t` keeps every `w`-second span around it at `max` records or fewer,
/// given the author's other records' times (`times`, any order).
pub fn window_ok(times: &[u64], t: u64, w: u64, max: u32) -> bool {
    if max == 0 {
        return false;
    }
    let mut near: Vec<u64> = times.iter().copied().filter(|x| x.abs_diff(t) < w).collect();
    near.push(t);
    near.sort_unstable();
    // every span [s, s + w) that contains t starts at a record time in (t - w, t]
    near.iter().filter(|s| **s <= t).all(|s| near.iter().filter(|x| **x >= *s && **x < s.saturating_add(w)).count() <= max as usize)
}

The window rolls: it isn't a calendar day. For a record at t, every w-second span that contains t must hold at most max records, counting this one. Only spans that start at a record time need checking. A property test feeds random times through it, accepting what fits, and checks that no day-long span of the accepted ones ever holds more than the budget.

  • Backdating doesn't dodge it. With two posts at 1,000 and 5,000 and a budget of 2 a day, a third at 3,000 fails, and so does one backdated to 900. The backdating_doesnt_dodge_the_rate_limit node test sends ten posts backdated an hour apart and accepts exactly the community's five a day.
  • What counts. Only records the store holds: refused records never count against the author. Records the store holds that are held back (beyond the author's standing) do count.
  • When it applies. On every record in a community except at startup load, from peers and from this device alike. Only a breach on live gossip strikes the key; a catch-up record over the limit is refused and nothing more.

The older token bucket, RateBucket (refills at a rate per day up to a capacity, then takes one), is still in hoi-validate for per-connection budgets; records use the windows.

Copy-paste floods

Spam is usually the same words, again and again, sometimes from many fresh keys. Each post and comment gets a fingerprint of its words:

words | — | sky | 6 | a post's title, text and link joined by newlines, or a comment's text normalized | — | mint | 6 | lowercased, whitespace collapsed to single spaces; under 12 characters: no fingerprint fingerprint | 16 B | sun | 6 | the first 16 bytes of BLAKE3 of the normalized words

The store indexes fingerprints per community: (author root, created_at, id) for every record with one. check_duplicate then refuses two patterns:

/// The same words twice from one author within a week, or from three newcomers within a day.
fn check_duplicate(&self, r: &Record, root: Id, c: Id) -> Result<(), Reject> {
    let Some(f) = hoi_validate::text_fingerprint(r) else { return Ok(()) };
    let same = self.store.same_text(&c, &f);
    if same.iter().any(|(a, t, _)| *a == root && t.abs_diff(r.created_at) < 7 * DAY) {
        return Err(Reject::Content("the same words again".into()));
    }
    if let Some(st) = self.communities.get(&c)
        && !st.is_admitted(&root)
    {
        let others: BTreeSet<Id> =
            same.iter().filter(|(a, t, _)| *a != root && t.abs_diff(r.created_at) < DAY && !st.is_admitted(a)).map(|x| x.0).collect();
        if others.len() >= 2 {
            return Err(Reject::Content("the same words from several newcomers: a copy-paste flood".into()));
        }
    }
    Ok(())
}

Case and spacing don't help: "Free crypto / click here now" and "free CRYPTO / click here\nnow" fingerprint the same. The copy_paste_floods_bounce test has three fresh keys post the same sentence: the first two are taken, the third is a flood. Admitted members can repeat each other freely; only an author repeating themselves within a week is refused. A refused duplicate from a peer is an Offence::Duplicate strike.

Strikes

Each device keeps a fading score per (key, community) in hoi_spam::Strikes. Every offence adds its weight; the score halves every hour. When a key crosses 10, the device bounces it and signs a report.

OffenceWeightRecorded whenReport reason
Rate1.0A record from a live gossip peer breaks a rate windowrate
Content3.0A record from a peer breaks the content rules; media bytes don't match what the post said or can't be decodedspam
Duplicate0.25A record from a peer is a copy-paste duplicatedup
 
pub const HALF_LIFE_SECS: f64 = 3600.0;
pub const BAN_AT: f64 = 10.0;

fn decay(score: f64, from: u64, to: u64) -> f64 {
    let dt = to.saturating_sub(from) as f64;
    score * 0.5_f64.powf(dt / HALF_LIFE_SECS)
}

impl Strikes {
    /// Records an offence by `key` in `community`. Returns true when the key just crossed the line.
    pub fn add(&mut self, key: Id, community: Id, o: Offence, now: u64) -> bool {
        let e = self.scores.entry((key, community)).or_insert((0.0, now));
        let before = decay(e.0, e.1, now);
        let after = before + o.weight();
        *e = (after, now);
        before < BAN_AT && after >= BAN_AT
    }
}

So the score at time t is s × 0.5^((t − t₀) / 3600), where s was the score after the last offence at t₀. A key crosses the line once: add returns true only on the offence that takes it from under 10 to 10 or more. Rate offences one second apart cross it on the eleventh (the score decays a little between them), and four content offences in quick succession cross it; spread out over hours, the same offences never do. In the spam_bounces_off test below, the eleventh refused post is the one that bounces the key. Scores below 0.05 are forgotten at the sweep. Ten hours after twelve quick rate offences, the score is under 0.1.

Who gets a strike

  • Only for what peers send. Strikes come only from records a peer delivered; a record refused on its way out of this device just fails with a message. This device's own person is never bounced.
  • Never for old rules. A record made before the rule it broke took effect is refused and nothing more: its author's client may have judged it by the rules of its day. The cutoff is hoi_validate::RULES_SINCE (0 in this build) for every offence and, for rate offences, also the time of the community's latest limits change.
  • Evidence. The device keeps up to 16 record ids per (key, community) as it records offences; when the key crosses the line, those ids become the report's evidence.

Bounces

A bounce is one device's verdict on a key, and the rules it judged by may differ from the author's client, so a bounce lifts on its own. The first lasts a day; each bounce of the same key while the last one is still remembered lasts twice as long, up to 30 days.

pub const BOUNCE_FOR: u64 = 86_400;
pub const BOUNCE_MAX: u64 = 30 * 86_400;

/// How long a key's `times`-th bounce in a row lasts.
pub fn bounce_lasts(times: u32) -> u64 {
    BOUNCE_FOR.saturating_mul(1 << times.saturating_sub(1).min(16)).min(BOUNCE_MAX)
}

A lifted bounce is remembered for 30 days (BOUNCE_MAX) after it lifts, then forgotten, so the next one starts at a day again. While a key is bounced on this device:

  • its new records are refused and counted, not stored; what was stored before stays stored, hidden, because deleting it would outlast the bounce;
  • its records aren't shown, and its votes don't count in tallies;
  • the community view lists it among the banned as bounced on this device: <reason>, and the Manage tab tags it "bounced automatically";
  • the device says so: "Bounced mallory for a day: too many bad records. Until then your device won't store or pass on anything it signs."

This device's own person is never bounced. unban on a key bounced only here lifts the bounce without signing anything.

Reports

A report is a signed record of kind report against a key. It is not a confidential message to moderators: anyone in the community can read it. Its evidence is record ids, never copies of the posts.

parentbytes32
The reported person's root key.
reasontext
spam, rate, dup, illegal or rule:<n>, at most 40 characters.
evidence[bytes32]
At most 64 record ids.

A report is 2 KiB at most, stamps at pow_bits − 8, shares the label rate group (20 a day for newcomers, 200 for members), and expires after the community's retention or 30 days, whichever is shorter. Reports come from two places:

  • Automatically, when a key crosses the strike line: reason rate, dup or spam from the offence that tipped it, with the evidence collected so far.
  • By hand, with flag(target, reason) on a post or comment: the report names its author, with the record as evidence.

Owners and moderators see reports on the Manage tab as flags: grouped by the flagged record when this device holds it, otherwise by the flagged key, with the reasons, how many people flagged, and their summed trust weight against the auto-bounce threshold. Flags can't be dismissed; they fade with time like everything else. A flag for illegal says a removal for that reason takes effect at once.

Weighted reports and autobans

Every time a report arrives, the device weighs all reports against that key in the community. Each reporter counts once, by the larger of their weight in this device's trust view and their standing in the community:

pub(crate) fn report_weight(&self, c: &Id, target: &Id) -> f64 {
    let st = self.communities.get(c);
    let mut by: BTreeMap<Id, f64> = BTreeMap::new();
    for s in self.store.of_kind(c, Kind::Report) {
        if s.record.parent == Some(*target) {
            let local = self.trust_level(&s.author_root).weight();
            let ladder = st.map_or(0.0, |st| match st.level(&s.author_root) {
                hoi_moderation::Level::Trusted => 1.0,
                hoi_moderation::Level::Established => 0.5,
                _ => 0.0,
            });
            by.insert(s.author_root, local.max(ladder));
        }
    }
    by.values().sum()
}

When the sum reaches the community's autoban_threshold (12 by default, 1–10,000), the device bounces the key with the reason enough trusted members flagged it. Owners and moderators are never autobanned. A pile of fresh keys can't ban anyone: they weigh almost nothing, and their reports are rate-limited like everything else.

On a moderator's device

On an owner's or moderator's device the ban also goes into the ledger, for everyone. After each batch of records and at each commit, write_autobans signs a ban entry with the reason Flagged by enough trusted members and up to 64 of the reports against the key as evidence. It does this once per person, skips anyone already banned and owners and moderators, and writes at most 200 a community an hour. The code says why that cap is not the safeguard: reports count only from established and trusted members (or friends), and the cap bounds a device gone wrong without stopping it answering a flood of fresh keys. The ledger ban can be vetoed like any other (vetoes).

lanes: Spammer | Your device | Moderator's device | Every device Spammer => Your device: 40 posts in a minute, live Your device: 2 taken (the newcomer budget); each one after is a rate strike Your device: the score crosses 10: bounced for a day Your device -> Every device: report {parent: the key, reason rate, evidence: record ids} Every device -> Moderator's device: more reports from established and trusted members Moderator's device: weighted reports reach autoban_threshold: bounce, ban due Moderator's device => Every device @coral: ledger {action ban, reason "Flagged by enough trusted members", evidence: report ids} Every device: the key's new records are refused here too

The spam_bounces_off test runs the first half: a fresh key sends 40 posts straight at a node with a newcomer budget of 2; exactly 2 are accepted, the key is bounced, its posts vanish from the feed, the community view lists it as bounced, and one signed report with evidence shows among the flags.

Peer scores

Keys sign records; peers forward them. A peer that forwards junk loses score and gets disconnected, so spam relays end up talking to themselves. hoi_spam::PeerScores keeps one score per connection, starting at 0.

pub const DISCONNECT_AT: f64 = -20.0;
/// The best score good behaviour earns. A penalty of `MAX_SCORE - DISCONNECT_AT` drops any peer.
pub const MAX_SCORE: f64 = 10.0;

impl PeerScores {
    /// Returns true when the peer should be dropped.
    pub fn penalize(&mut self, peer: &str, amount: f64) -> bool {
        let s = self.scores.entry(peer.to_string()).or_insert(0.0);
        *s -= amount;
        *s <= DISCONNECT_AT
    }

    pub fn reward(&mut self, peer: &str) {
        let s = self.scores.entry(peer.to_string()).or_insert(0.0);
        *s = (*s + 0.1).min(MAX_SCORE);
    }
}

Other parts of the node (carrying, injecting, scattered pieces, signaling, linking) penalize their own protocol violations by 1 to 5 the same way. At −20 or below the node says Bye with a reason (too many invalid records, media that doesn't match its hash, too many messages) and closes the link. A forged identity costs an Argon2id run to check, which is why one is one too many. Refusals that don't point at the peer, such as a stamp too weak, a rate limit or a label from an unfollowed labeler, cost it nothing: an older peer may still send them in good faith. A peer's score is forgotten when it disconnects.

What the simulator's attacks show

hoi-sim attacks real hoi-core nodes the way an attacker's own tool would: no client in the way, no rules obeyed on the sending side (simulator). A Forger owns a root identity with the cheapest acceptable work (4 bits) and a blessed device, and can sign anything, well-formed or not.

The siege

scenario::siege runs a founder, honest members and attackers around a hub node over accelerated time, with an 8-bit stamp, a one-day established_age, one vouch to get in, 4 newcomer posts a day and a one-hour contest window. Honest members act through the ordinary node API; everything hostile goes in as raw records over the sync protocol. At the end it scores these invariants:

  1. Honest members become established

    by being vouched in and waiting.

  2. Forged records never reach the feed

    Every catalogued forgery, from every attacker, injected straight at the hub; none is displayed.

  3. Spam is held for newcomers and the community naps the rest without a moderator

    Five spam posts from each fresh key; whatever shows, honest members hide, and none is left showing.

  4. A Sybil ring vouched in together shares one voucher's voice

    A turncoat vouches six Sybils in at once; together they hold no more voice than the turncoat.

  5. A Sybil mob can't block a real member

    Every Sybil hides a loyal member's posts and objects to them; the member stands.

  6. Rules and limits change only by consent

    The founder proposes raising identity work to 16 bits; it stays pending.

  7. Honest nodes agree

    on every member's level. With churn, members who came and went catch up with everything.

CI runs the siege with 3 honest members and 2 attackers, 5 and 4, and 6 and 3 with a third of members offline at any time; a run with 200 honest nodes, 20 attackers and 20% churn is ignored by default and runs when asked for (--ignored).

What this can't do

  • Bounces and strikes are per device. Each device judges what reaches it. A key bounced on yours may post fine elsewhere until other devices see the same behavior, or until the community's ledger bans it.
  • A modified client can ignore all of it on its own side. It can't make other devices accept what breaks their windows or rules: that is the point of checking on receipt.
  • Patient spammers. A key that stays inside every window and writes different words each time isn't spam to these checks. It is a newcomer posting text, marked unvalidated, which established members can hide and block (hides, blocks).
  • Identity work is a cost, not a wall. It makes keys expensive in bulk; it doesn't stop one determined person with time.
