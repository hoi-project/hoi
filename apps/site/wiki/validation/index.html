Validation pipeline

Every record a device stores, shows or passes on goes through one function first: Node::ingest. It runs the same stages whether the record was typed on this device, arrived live from a peer, came in during catch-up, or was read back from disk. This page walks the stages in the order the code runs them, names every refusal, and says who pays for it.

Source hoi-core/src/node.rs · hoi-validate/src/lib.rs · hoi-spam/src/lib.rs · hoi-core/src/net.rs · hoi-core/src/work.rs

One function, every record

The checks are split in two. hoi-validate holds the pure stages: functions of the record bytes and a few numbers, with no state (decode, stamp, signature, delegation_consent, delegation, identity, time, rate_windows, window_ok, content). Node::ingest in hoi-core/src/node.rs composes them with what the device knows: the store (identities, delegations, rotations, burns), each community's moderation state from hoi-moderation, and the strike counters from hoi-spam.

The order is the point. The cheapest checks run first, so a flood of junk costs a hash per record and never reaches a signature check, let alone an 8 MiB Argon2id run. A record that fails a stage stops there: nothing later runs, nothing is stored, nothing is passed on.

Records enter on the left and pass decode, stamp floor, signature, stamp, identity, time, moderation, rate and content in turn. A malformed record stops at decode, an unstamped one at the stamp floor, a forged one at the signature, a flood at the rate stage; the good ones are accepted, and a newcomer's link is stored but held back.

The stages in the order Node::ingest runs them. Cheap checks first; a record stops at the first stage it fails.

Where a record comes from

Every call to ingest names a Source. The source changes a few stages (which are skipped, whether a record must be fresh, whether anyone is blamed), never their order.

There is no separate gossip message. Live records and catch-up both arrive in recs messages on the sync link (Sync & gossip), and the receiver decides per record: live = created_at + CLOCK_SKEW >= now, with CLOCK_SKEW = 600 seconds. Records that come in through the injector of an open community count as live too.

Standing is not a stage that refuses

A record that is valid but beyond its author's standing (a newcomer's link, a member's picture) passes every stage and is stored. Standing decides afterwards whether anyone but its author sees it and whether it is passed on (Standing and holds).

1 · Decode

hoi_validate::decode refuses anything over 64 KiB + 256 bytes before parsing, so a huge blob never reaches the CBOR decoder. Then Wire::decode reads the wire map {r, sig} strictly: canonical DAG-CBOR only (shortest integers, definite lengths, sorted text keys, no floats, no tags), every field present and typed, no unknown keys, a known kind, protocol version 1, and the shape rules of Record::check_shape (which kinds carry a community and a parent, a genesis signed by its own key, a delegation that expires with its record, and so on). The id is BLAKE3 of the record's canonical bytes, computed here once. Last, the encoded size must fit the kind's cap.

pub fn decode(bytes: &[u8]) -> Result<Decoded, Reject> {
    if bytes.len() > 64 * 1024 + 256 {
        return Err(Reject::TooBig);
    }
    let (wire, id) = Wire::decode(bytes).map_err(|e| Reject::Decode(e.to_string()))?;
    if bytes.len() > wire.record.kind().max_size() {
        return Err(Reject::TooBig);
    }
    Ok(Decoded { wire, id, size: bytes.len() })
}

2 · Gates before any signature

Four questions are answered from the decoded record and the device's state, still without verifying anything:

  1. Private communities take the encrypted path

    A record of a community this device knows to be private, arriving as Peer or Import, is refused: NotAllowed("Private community records require the encrypted group connection."). A Local record for a private community this device can no longer write to (its encrypted membership ended) is refused too, unless it is the community's own genesis signed with a community key this device holds. See Private communities.

  2. Already here

    If the store holds the id, the answer is Duplicate, with three side effects: a copy from a peer bumps the stored record's from_peers count; one of this device's own records that comes back from a peer is offered like any other from then on (the injector's hold ends); and a complete public copy can clear a record's private-carrier mark. No blame either way.

  3. Removed or suppressed

    Ids whose bodies were deleted (removed, deleted by their author, superseded, burned), ids kept as a "Poof" skeleton, and records or communities the node's operator suppressed are refused as Tombstoned, however they arrive. A removed post stays gone even if a peer still offers it.

  4. Joined and known

    A record for a community this device has not joined is refused, NotAllowed("not subscribed to this community"). If the community is joined but its genesis hasn't arrived yet, anything but the genesis is refused as UnknownCommunity. A stranger's records for communities this device doesn't hold cost one hash and nothing more.

3 · The stamp floor

Before the signature, the record's proof of work is checked against a floor: the least any key could ever need for this kind here. Checking a stamp is one BLAKE3 hash of id ‖ u64be(nonce) (Stamps & work), much cheaper than an Ed25519 verification, so unstamped junk is turned away for one hash each. Records read back at startup (Load) skip it.

pub fn stamp_floor(kind: Kind) -> u32 {
    match kind {
        Kind::Community | Kind::Delegation | Kind::Revoke | Kind::Identity | Kind::Rotate => hoi_types::GLOBAL_POW_BITS,
        k => 8u32.saturating_sub(k.pow_discount()).saturating_sub(6),
    }
}

The protocol-wide floor assumes the lowest difficulty a community may set (8 bits), minus the kind's discount and the largest key-age discount (6). For a community the device holds, ingest raises it to that community's own floor: required_bits(pow_bits, kind discount, 366 days), the stamp a key older than a year would need, under the most lenient limits that apply to the record's time.

KindProtocol floorFloor in a community at the default 22 bits
community, delegation, revoke, identity, rotate16 bits16 bits (they belong to no community; a genesis is always 16)
post, comment, edit, pointer2 bits16 bits (22 − 6)
Kinds with the 8-bit discount: vote, label, ledger, report, delete, vouch, review, profile, profile-color, roster, share, trust, wiki, tally, blocklist0 bits8 bits (22 − 8 − 6)

4 · The signature

hoi_validate::signature verifies sig over the 32-byte record id with the record's author key, using ed25519-dalek's verify_strict. Strict verification refuses non-canonical signatures and small-order points, so one signature can't be made to verify for many messages. A failure is BadSignature, and the peer that sent it is blamed.

A valid signature only says the author key signed it. Who that key speaks for is the next stage.

5 · Who is speaking

Node::speaker turns the signing key into a person: the root key the record counts for. It keeps identities apart, so nobody can claim someone else's device and a replaced or burned root can't speak. The rules depend on the body:

Two signatures on a delegation

A delegation is signed by the device it blesses: that is the device's consent. Inside it, root_sig is the root's blessing over delegation_statement(root, device, scope, expires_at). delegation_consent checks that both keys are strong (valid curve points outside the small-order subgroup), that the root really signed the statement, and that the blessing lasts at most MAX_DELEGATION, 400 days, past the record's created_at.

pub fn delegation(record: &Record, deleg: Option<&DelegationInfo>) -> Result<Id, Reject> {
    let d = deleg.ok_or(Reject::NoDelegation)?;
    if record.created_at > d.expires_at {
        return Err(Reject::DelegationExpired);
    }
    if let Some(rv) = d.revoked_at
        && record.created_at >= rv
    {
        return Err(Reject::Revoked);
    }
    let needed = scope_needed(record.kind(), record.community.as_ref()).ok_or(Reject::Scope)?;
    if !scope_covers(&d.scope, &needed) {
        return Err(Reject::Scope);
    }
    Ok(d.root)
}

Both tests use the record's own created_at, so a device's records from before its revocation stay valid everywhere. The scope a device needs for each kind:

Right after the speaker is known

  • Burned. If the person's root was burned, everything they sign is Burned, except the burn record itself.
  • A genesis never changes. A second community record for a key that already has one is NotAllowed.
  • Strangers' keys. Identities, delegations and revocations have no community and are kept up to a hundred years. From a peer that is not one of this person's own devices nor their home node, they are taken only for roots this device already knows (it holds their identity or a device of theirs, records of theirs in a community it carries, or they own or moderate a community here), for roots the same batch carries community records of, or for a new root that continues someone known. Otherwise: NotAllowed("an identity, device or revocation of a key nobody here has heard from"). Without this rule anyone could fill every node with keys nobody uses, for good.
  • Sixteen live devices. A new delegation for a root that already has MAX_LIVE_DEVICES (16) live, unrevoked devices is NotAllowed. Renewals of a known device, this person's own root, and records loaded from disk are exempt.

6 · The full stamp

Now the stamp is checked against what this community actually asks of this key. bits_for applies required_bits(pow_bits, kind discount, key age) for records in a community this device holds, and GLOBAL_POW_BITS (16) for everything else, genesis included. Records loaded from disk need 0.

pub fn required_bits(pow_bits: u32, kind_discount: u32, key_age_secs: u64) -> u32 {
    let d = key_age_secs / 86_400;
    let age = if d < 30 { 0 } else if d < 90 { 2 } else if d < 365 { 4 } else { 6 };
    pow_bits.saturating_sub(kind_discount).saturating_sub(age)
}

Key age counts from first_seen: when this device first accepted anything from that person, never from what a record claims. Nobody can backdate their way to a discount, and two devices may disagree about a key's age. That is why an author stamps for the full difficulty: bits_to_stamp is bits_for(record, 0).

The difficulty is limits_for(community, created_at): the lower of the community's current pow_bits and the one in effect when the record says it was made. Records members took under looser limits stay valid on devices that join after the limits tightened. The same rule gives the longer retention, the higher newcomer rate and the lower identity work.

7 · Identity work

Two checks, both about the person's memory-hard work (Stamps & work):

  1. An identity record's own work

    For an identity record, hoi_validate::identity refuses weak keys and runs one Argon2id evaluation (8 MiB, one pass) over root ‖ u64be(work). Fewer than MIN_BITS (4) leading zero bits is BadIdentity. The answer is cached per (root, work) in the node's state, so each nonce is checked once.

  2. The author's identity in this community

    For every other record in a community (not the genesis, not on load), the person's best identity record must exist (NoIdentity) and carry at least the community's ladder.identity_bits (default 8; WeakIdentity { have, need }). "Best" is the most work along the person's chain of roots, so a person whose root was replaced keeps the work they did.

Argon2id is the only expensive step, so it comes after the stamp and the signature. Native hosts keep the node behind a lock that every connection waits for; work.rs lets them check a recs message's identity records first, outside the lock: scan (stamp floor and signature only), ask the node under a short lock which ones are new, run Argon2id with no lock held, stop at the first forgery, then hand the message in with the answers for the pipeline to look up. The order of the stages doesn't change; the browser's worker has no lock and checks inline.

Forged work ends the link

A BadIdentity from a peer costs that peer MAX_SCORE − DISCONNECT_AT (30) points, enough to disconnect any peer at once, and ingest_batch stops taking the rest of its batch: nothing more from it is worth another Argon2id run.

8 · Time

pub fn time(record: &Record, now: u64, live: bool, max_retention: Option<u64>) -> Result<(), Reject> {
    if record.created_at > now + CLOCK_SKEW {
        return Err(Reject::FromTheFuture);
    }
    if live && record.created_at + CLOCK_SKEW < now {
        return Err(Reject::TooOld);
    }
    if record.expires_at <= now {
        return Err(Reject::Expired);
    }
    let cap = max_retention.unwrap_or(KEEP);
    if record.expires_at > record.created_at.saturating_add(cap) {
        return Err(Reject::ExpiryTooFar);
    }
    Ok(())
}

How far in the future a record may say it was made, and how old a live record may be.
livebool
Source::Peer { live: true }, except a ledger entry carrying co-signatures (a threshold proposal), which may travel for a while before enough owners sign.
max_retentionOption<u64>
The community's max_retention (180 days by default) under limits_for; None means KEEP, a hundred years, for records outside the retention window.

Kept outside the window (exempt_from_retention): the genesis, delegations, revocations, identities, rotations, vouches, shares, wiki edits, and ledger entries kept until superseded (rules, limits, moderators). A review that answers a proposal is kept as long as the proposal, since the answers decide it. Threshold proposals made here or arriving live are refused as TooOld once they are more than PROPOSAL_MAX_AGE (7 days) old. Records that pass carry their own end: past expires_at, every device deletes them (Expiry & removal).

9 · Moderation state

For a community this device holds, read from the community's ledger as hoi-moderation settled it (Communities & the ledger):

  • Banned. A ban on the person counts for records made at or after the ban: Banned. What they wrote before stays.
  • Removed. If a removal of this very record is in effect (its contest window passed, or it was urgent), the id is marked deleted until the record's own expiry and the record is Tombstoned.
  • Bounced here. If this device bounced the person (strikes) and the bounce hasn't lifted, the record is refused as Banned and counted in bounced_records. A bounce never applies to this person's own records or to loading: what was stored before a bounce stays stored (and hidden) over a restart, since deleting it would outlast the bounce.

10 · Rate

Each key may have so many records of a kind in a community within rolling windows. The windows count records by their own created_at, not by arrival, so every device reaches the same answer, and backdated records arriving as catch-up can't skip the limit. Owners and moderators are exempt, and the check is skipped for records loaded from disk. "Newcomer" means not yet admitted on the community's ladder (Standing & review).

Kinds count in groups (rate_group): edits and deletes share one, vouches and reviews another, labels and reports a third. window_ok takes the author's other record times in that group and accepts a new one only if every window-long span containing it holds at most the limit, wherever the new record lands in time. A limit of 0 accepts nothing.

pub fn window_ok(times: &[u64], t: u64, w: u64, max: u32) -> bool {
    if max == 0 {
        return false;
    }
    let mut near: Vec<u64> = times.iter().copied().filter(|x| x.abs_diff(t) < w).collect();
    near.push(t);
    near.sort_unstable();
    // every span [s, s + w) that contains t starts at a record time in (t - w, t]
    near.iter().filter(|s| **s <= t).all(|s| near.iter().filter(|x| **x >= *s && **x < s.saturating_add(w)).count() <= max as usize)
}

One key's posts on a timeline, with an hour window and a day window sliding over them. A post that would put more than the limit inside any window-long span is dropped.

A key's posts against rolling windows; the over-budget ones are dropped, wherever their created_at puts them.

Only a live record from a peer that breaks a window earns its key a strike (weight 1). Catch-up records over the limit are refused without blame. More on Spam & rate limits.

11 · Content and copy-paste floods

hoi_validate::content checks the body of every kind: text through the rules of Text, links & names, media references through those of Media, and the shape of every other field (a vote is −1, 0 or 1; an objection carries one of the allowed reasons; a delegation's scope is known; a community has 1–16 owners and a threshold between 1 and their number; limits within range; a share is 81 bytes per device; and so on). Any failure is Content(message). From a peer, it costs the author's key a strike of weight 3.

Then, for posts and comments in a community (not on load), check_duplicate looks for copy-paste floods using text_fingerprint: the words lowercased, runs of whitespace collapsed, BLAKE3, first 16 bytes. Texts shorter than 12 characters after that have no fingerprint.

  • The same words from the same person within 7 days: Content("the same words again").
  • From a newcomer, the same words that two or more other newcomers used within a day: Content("the same words from several newcomers: a copy-paste flood").

A flood from a peer costs the key a strike of weight 0.25 (Offence::Duplicate).

Two more steps before the record is stored:

  • Public joins. If the person is joining this community from a directory result, its genesis must say admission: open and its name must match the result; otherwise the join is undone and the genesis is NotAllowed.
  • Supersession. Answers to a proposal are all kept, up to MAX_ANSWERS (16) per person; one more is NotAllowed.

13 · Accept, and what follows

A record that reached here is stored as a Stored entry: its id, record, signature, the exact wire bytes, the person it counts for (author_root), when it arrived, how many peers sent it, and the stamp and identity bits it carried. Then, in order:

  1. Late judgements

    A vouch, review or ledger entry from a peer, an import, the private relay or pieces that says it was made more than LATE_GRACE (one hour) before this device last caught up with the community counts from when it turned up, not from its created_at. Nobody wins a decision by backdating.

  2. Provenance

    Records without a community that came through the private relay are marked as private carriers; media of posts in private communities is marked private unless the same bytes were public before. Raw sync never carries either.

  3. Store and persist

    The record goes into the in-memory index and a Change::Put is queued for storage (not on load). Records it supersedes are dropped. Trust statements and blocklists mark the friend ring and the followed lists for recomputing.

  4. Note the author

    first_seen is set for the person if it wasn't. Profile pictures and colors update their heads. A post's or comment's name becomes the person's display name if it is the newest, and a name that is a domain is queued for a handle check.

  5. Kind effects

    after_accept: a genesis creates the community's state; a delete turns its parent into a skeleton ("deleted by the author"); new rules or limits from a peer tell an established person they have a say; a report checks the autoban weight; a burn purges everything the burned root signed; a pointer is taken in for its object.

  6. Settle and pass on

    The community is marked dirty for the next settle. This device's own new record goes through an injector where one is linked, and is kept from its own links until it comes back from elsewhere. Then it is gossiped to every peer that shares the community, except the one it came from, together with its carriers (the author's identity and device blessing, and a replaced person's chain), unless it is held, private, kept in pieces, or came through the private relay or pieces.

14 · Standing and holds

A stored record may still be held back: shown to nobody but its author, passed on to nobody, until its author's standing allows it. Node::held answers why, or None. It runs whenever the record is shown, counted or offered, so a hold lifts on its own the moment the author earns the standing.

The author is visible here, and the record isn't held, or this person wrote it.

Usable, not kept in pieces, not held by the injector: it may be offered and gossiped.

When a community settles, this person's own records that were held and no longer are (an age proof was accepted, a vouch arrived) are gossiped right away rather than waiting for the next sync. The app also refuses up front what a person's standing wouldn't let out: check_standing stops a newcomer's link or a member's media before anything is stamped.

"Key" blame is not applied uniformly: the node strikes for RateLimited only on live peer records, and for Content on any peer record. Records made on this device, loaded, imported or rebuilt never strike anyone.

What failing costs

The peer that sent it

Each connection has a score in hoi-spam's PeerScores. An accepted record rewards the peer 0.1, up to MAX_SCORE (10). Penalties on the sync link:

What the peer didPenalty
Wires in a recs message that don't decode at all2 each, counted before any is taken in
A record refused with peer blame (Decode, TooBig, BadSignature)2
Forged identity work (BadIdentity)30, so any peer is dropped
Media bytes that don't hash to what they claim5

At DISCONNECT_AT (−20) the node says bye ("too many invalid records", or "media that doesn't match its hash") and closes the link. Peers that forward junk talk to fewer and fewer devices.

The key that signed it

hoi-spam's Strikes keep a score per (key, community), halving every hour (HALF_LIFE_SECS = 3600):

OffenceWeightWhen
Rate1a live peer record over a rate window
Content3a peer record failing the content rules; media bytes that hash right but fail the checks or can't be decoded
Duplicate0.25a copy-paste flood from a peer

Up to 16 offending record ids are kept as evidence. When the score crosses BAN_AT (10), this device bounces the person: for a day the first time (BOUNCE_FOR), twice as long each time again while the last bounce is remembered, never more than BOUNCE_MAX (30 days); a lifted bounce is remembered for 30 days. It tells its person ("Bounced … for a day: too many bad records. Until then your device won't store or pass on anything it signs.") and publishes a signed report with reason rate, dup or spam and the evidence ids, never the posts themselves. A bounce is one device's verdict and lifts on its own; reports from members with weight can lead to a community ban (Spam & rate limits).

Old records under new rules

A record made before the rule it broke took effect is refused and nothing more: its author's client may have judged it by the rules of its day. strikes_from is RULES_SINCE (0 in this build) and, for rate offences, the time the community's current limits took effect.

Batches

Sync messages, bundles and imports go through ingest_batch. It first notes which roots the batch carries community records of (device keys resolved through the batch's own delegations), so their identities may come along, then takes records in an order that puts dependencies first, by (priority(kind), created_at):

PriorityKinds
0–6community, identity, rotate, delegation, revoke, ledger, vouch
7–17post, comment, edit, review, vote, label, report, delete, profile, profile-color, pointer
18–23roster, share, trust, wiki, tally, blocklist

Before the first record of priority 7 (SETTLE_BEFORE), the ladder is settled, so people admitted by vouches in the same batch post with their new standing. After the batch: settle again, catch up a recovery rotation if this device is restoring, and write any autobans due on a moderator's device.
