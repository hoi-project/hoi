Mailboxes & encrypted relays

Members of a private group never hand each other messages directly. Each device leaves sealed, padded MLS messages where the others will look: in numbered slots on a few public nodes the group agreed on (mailboxes), or, on a build that names one, in an opaque encrypted relay. Both carry the same objects and neither learns the community, its members or a message's exact size. This page covers both sides of each: the core's mailbox.rs and pickup.rs, the web app's relay client, and the relay service in relay/.

Not the other relays

The site's Run a relay page is about something else: a desktop app or hoi-node keeping public communities reachable for members who are away, and relays that pass encrypted QUIC packets between devices (Transports). That page notes that private communities use a separate encrypted relay; this page is about that one, and about mailboxes.

Padded sizes

Both sides use one rule: at least 4 KiB, at most four significant bits, at most 1 MiB. That gives 65 sizes, at most one eighth above the content past 4 KiB. The relay refuses every other size; the core refuses to open one.

/** Stored sizes keep at most four significant bits: 4 KiB, 4.5 KiB … 7.5 KiB, 8 KiB, 9 KiB … 1 MiB. */
export function isPaddedSize(size) {
  return Number.isSafeInteger(size) && size >= MIN_OBJECT_BYTES && size <= MAX_OBJECT_BYTES && size % (1 << (28 - Math.clz32(size))) === 0;
}

Mailboxes

A group's mailboxes are holders: public nodes that keep pieces for others and answer sealed requests (Scattered delivery). To a holder a mailbox slot is just a piece: the same sealed put, has and get, the same stamp, the same rule that nothing is overwritten. Browsers cannot be holders; nodes and desktops can.

The list in the group context

The list lives in the MLS group context under extension type 0xff44, as canonical CBOR. Only a commit changes it, so every member of an epoch sees the same list, and a Welcome hands it to a newcomer.

vuint
1; anything else reads as no list.
marray, at most 8
Mailboxes: {e: endpoint (32 bytes), r: [route text]}, at most 4 routes of at most 128 bytes each.
d32 bytes, optional
The group's drop key (Ed25519, public part), which pieces of its posts are stored with.
harray, optional
The committer devices the drop key's secret was given to, at most 64. Present only with d.

Anything malformed reads as no list. Older readers ignore d and h.

Choosing and keeping them

The owner's device keeps the list; in a group whose membership follows its records, the committing devices do, in their order and turns (Private communities), and change it only once they have caught up with the epoch.

  • Keep every listed mailbox that is linked now or was seen within MAILBOX_STALE (2 days); the list is fine as it is when all are alive and there are 4.
  • Fill up to MAILBOXES (4): holders this device saw long and steadily first, then any reachable holder with routes; each in a place of its own (a network, or one named operator, is one place), with up to 4 routes members can dial.
  • Commit the new list only if it has at least MIN_MAILBOXES (2) and differs. The commit goes in the epoch it ends, so members reading the old slots learn the new ones.
  • Look again every 600 s, or every 30 s while the group has fewer than 2. Meanwhile the host dials more holders: mailboxes in use first, then holders met before (seen within 2 days, steadiest first, up to 8) and public nodes from the directory.

A group of one has nobody to read its messages: objects that got no slot are acknowledged at once.

Slots, writes and the bell

Each member writes its messages of an epoch into its own numbered slots on every mailbox. Only members of that epoch can compute where a slot is; each membership change starts a fresh set; holders see random locators, never a listing.

/// Where member `leaf`'s slot `seq` of an epoch is.
pub(crate) fn slot(key: &[u8; 32], leaf: u32, seq: u32) -> [u8; 32] {
    let mut msg = [0u8; 8];
    msg[..4].copy_from_slice(&leaf.to_be_bytes());
    msg[4..].copy_from_slice(&seq.to_be_bytes());
    *blake3::keyed_hash(key, &msg).as_bytes()
}

/// The slot key of an epoch, from its relay secret.
pub(crate) fn slot_key(relay_secret: &[u8; 32]) -> [u8; 32] {
    blake3::derive_key("hoi mailbox slot v1", relay_secret)
}

Writing

  1. A slot per message

    Every object of the current room that has no slot gets this device's next sequence number in this epoch (assign_slots), in outbox order. A membership change may follow at once once everything ahead of it has a slot.

  2. Put on every mailbox

    A put sealed to the holder's key (envelopes): the locator, a one-time reply key, an expiry day of min(max(expires, now + 1 h), now + 30 days) rounded up to a day, a drop hash from this device's own mailbox drop secret, a stamp mined for this holder, locator, length and day, and the sealed object. At most 32 puts per group are out at once.

  3. Count it sent

    Once WRITE_QUORUM (2) mailboxes stored it, or every mailbox answered and at least one stored it, the object is acknowledged and leaves the outbox.

  4. Never overwritten

    A holder that already keeps something else under the locator answers Taken (this device lost its place after a restart): the message moves to the next slot of its epoch, if the epoch is still current. Other refusals mark that mailbox done.

  5. Ring the bell

    Once stored, the writer rings its epoch's bell on every mailbox: 32 random bytes at the next free slot of leaf u32::MAX, which no member has, kept for 2 days. A Taken ring moves to the next bell slot, so two members ringing at once take two.

Reading

  • The bell. An idle member probes only the bell, on one mailbox chosen by its own leaf index, so readers spread over the mailboxes. Every writer rings on all of them, so one is enough.
  • A ring sends the member over every other member's next slot, three probes each (CHASE): has answers in a 64-byte envelope with the piece's length, then get asks for exactly that length. A member found writing is followed until it stops.
  • A sweep every 600 s looks at every member anyway, and one past the bell's next slot, in case a ring or a slot was lost.
  • In order. A fetched slot goes through privateReceive like any object. A commit found past a member's next slot is held, not followed, until the slots before it are read. A member whose next slot keeps missing is looked at one past it on every fourth miss; once something was found ahead and the next slot missed 4 times, the reader counts it lost and moves on (following a held commit then).
  • Retry. A message that came before what it depends on (an attachment before its post) gets another try for an hour, up to 64 waiting.
lanes: Writer | Mailbox A | Mailbox B | Reader Writer: object gets slot s of leaf w, epoch e Writer -> Mailbox A: put loc(w, s), stamp, sealed object Writer -> Mailbox B: put loc(w, s), stamp, sealed object Mailbox A --> Writer: stored ok Mailbox B --> Writer: stored ok Writer: quorum of 2: acknowledged Writer -> Mailbox A: ring: 32 random bytes at bell slot b Writer -> Mailbox B: ring: 32 random bytes at bell slot b Reader -> Mailbox B: has bell slot b Mailbox B --> Reader: had: rang Reader -> Mailbox A: has loc(w, s) Mailbox A --> Reader: had, its length Reader -> Mailbox A: get loc(w, s), that length Mailbox A => Reader: got: the sealed object Reader: privateReceive; next look at slot s+1 at once

Reaching a mailbox

Requests to holders travel as piece messages on ordinary sync links (Sync & gossip): {op, rid, to?, env}, where env is sealed to the holder and the answer is sealed to a one-time key the request carries. For each mailbox the device picks a way (mail_links):

  • Through a forwarder in another place that vouches for the holder: the forwarder passes the envelope on (to names the holder) and the answer back. The holder sees the forwarder's address, never this device's; the forwarder sees only envelopes. Mailboxes reached this way are not dialed.
  • Over its own link to the holder, when no such forwarder is around. The holder then sees this device's address.

The host keeps one link per mailbox in use (up to 4), and dials the routes the lists give for mailboxes it does not reach through a forwarder. Before linking, the app asks the person to accept the holder's operator notice, as for any node (hoi-node); the end-to-end test accepts both holders' notices this way.

Picking up an invitation

The device that adds a newcomer is not always the one the newcomer talked to: in a group whose membership follows its records, a member passes the request on and a committer adds them later. So every join request carries a one-time X25519 key and up to 4 holders the newcomer's app reaches (its own choice; it knows no mailbox of the group yet). The committer that adds the newcomer seals the invitation to that key and stores it on those holders, at numbered slots:

/// Where slot `i` of a pickup key is.
pub(crate) fn pickup_loc(key: &[u8; 32], i: u32) -> [u8; 32] {
    *blake3::keyed_hash(&blake3::derive_key("hoi invitation pickup v1", key), &i.to_be_bytes()).as_bytes()
}

The relay client in the web app

Which relays

Relay URLs are build-time policy, never taken from an invite. scripts/relay-config.mjs reads the relay addresses, and the Vite build defines it as __HOI_RELAY_URLS__ and adds each origin to the Content-Security-Policy's connect-src. The default is an empty list.

/** Explicit relay URLs are build-time policy, never supplied by an untrusted invite. */
export function relayUrls(value, fallback = []) {
  const values = value === undefined ? fallback : JSON.parse(value);
  if (!Array.isArray(values) || values.length > 3 || values.some(item => typeof item !== 'string')) {
    throw new Error('… must be a JSON array of at most three relay base URLs.');
  }
  return [...new Set(values.map(value => {
    if (value.replace(/\/+$/, '') === '/relay') return '/relay';
    const url = new URL(value);
    const loopback = ['localhost', '127.0.0.1', '[::1]'].includes(url.hostname);
    if (url.protocol !== 'https:' && !(url.protocol === 'http:' && loopback)) throw new Error('Relay URLs require HTTPS except on loopback.');
    if (url.username || url.password || url.search || url.hash) throw new Error('Relay URLs cannot contain credentials, queries or fragments.');
    return url.href.replace(/\/+$/, '');
  }))];
}

Each URL is the complete API base, including /relay (for example https://relay.example/relay); /relay alone means the app's own origin.

One sync

syncPrivateGroup(core, community) in private-relay.ts. The core signs every read and upload; the client only moves bytes and checks them.

  1. Room

    Ask the core for the group. No relay configured: "Private syncing is unavailable. Your data stays on this device." Not active: stop.

  2. List

    For each relay, page through objects?after=<cursor>, at most 64 pages. Each page gets its own read signature from privateRead, made just before the request, since the relay accepts each once. A page is at most 64 KiB of JSON and 64 entries; each entry needs a 64-hex id, a sequence above the cursor and a size from 4 KiB to 1 MiB; at most 4096 objects per room per relay; nextCursor must equal the last sequence.

  3. Fetch

    Objects not yet received get read signatures in one call and are downloaded, each bounded to 1 MiB, at most 64 MiB per sync. A body whose length or SHA-256 does not match is not passed on.

  4. Receive

    Hand each object to privateReceive, up to 8 passes while one makes progress: replicas list one epoch in different orders, and a message may wait for one before it. Failures are retried, never read any other way.

  5. Follow commits

    If a commit moved the group to a new room, repeat from the list step in the new room, up to 8 epochs per sync. What is left in the old room belongs to an epoch the device no longer reads.

  6. Upload

    Take the outbox; check each object's room, size and hash; PUT it to every relay with X-Hoi-Expires and X-Hoi-Signature; the receipt (at most 4 KiB) must repeat the id, size and expiry. Only objects every configured relay stored are acknowledged; the rest stay for retry.

Every request uses credentials: 'omit', referrerPolicy: 'no-referrer', cache: 'no-store', redirect: 'error' and a 15 s timeout, and stops at once when the app locks. One relay failing does not stop the others.

When the app syncs

The app runs syncPrivateGroups every 15 s and 750 ms after a changed event, only on a build with relays, only with an identity, and only for active groups whose transport is not mailboxes; one sync runs per group at a time. For a group on mailboxes (or a build without relays), "sync" only asks the core for its outbox, which gives anything new its slot. The status line says "Encrypted updates checked with n of m relays.", or "Some relay copies are unavailable. Your changes remain saved for retry.", "Some encrypted updates could not be verified yet. Existing messages are unchanged.", or, for a removed device, "This device was removed. New messages are no longer available here."

Relay protocol version 2

The relay checks room signatures, hashes, padded sizes, quotas and expiry. It does not decrypt messages, decide membership, moderate plaintext or authorize changes to signed community records. A room is an Ed25519 public key; any current member of the epoch can sign for it and none is named.

Rooms

Each MLS epoch of a group has its own room. Every member exports a 32-byte relay secret from the epoch (MLS exporter, label hoi relay v2, empty context); BLAKE3 derive_key("hoi relay v2 room key", secret) seeds an Ed25519 key whose public half is the room id. Every member of the epoch derives the same key and signs with it, so the relay sees one key per room and no member's device or identity key. Knowing the community key, or holding an invitation meant for someone else, derives nothing. A commit is uploaded to the room of the epoch it ends; a newcomer reads the new room, where members resend the live history they keep; a removed device reads its removal in the old room and cannot derive the next one (Private communities).

Expired ciphertext may be uploaded again only with a fresh envelope.

target is the request below the room exactly as sent: objects?after=<sequence> or objects/<id>. The core signs only those two shapes (a cursor of 1 to 15 digits without a leading zero, or a 64-hex id), at most 65 per call, each with a fresh 16-byte nonce. The window is inclusive on both sides. The relay accepts each read once: it keeps SHA-256("hoi relay read v2\n<room>\n<nonce>") until the window closes and refuses the same nonce again, also after a restart. The digest does not name the room. A signature for one room opens no other; anyone holding a room key may list it, even when it is empty.

What the relay stores and logs

One Durable Object, named opaque-objects, holds the entire service and enforces its totals. Its SQLite schema:

this.sql.exec(`CREATE TABLE IF NOT EXISTS objects_v2 (
  sequence INTEGER PRIMARY KEY AUTOINCREMENT,
  room TEXT NOT NULL, id TEXT NOT NULL, expires INTEGER NOT NULL, size INTEGER NOT NULL CHECK(size > 0), body BLOB NOT NULL,
  UNIQUE(room, id)
) STRICT`);
// One digest per accepted read, kept only while its signature could still be replayed.
this.sql.exec('CREATE TABLE IF NOT EXISTS reads (digest TEXT PRIMARY KEY, expires INTEGER NOT NULL) STRICT');
// Identifiers an operator removed (id '' is a whole room), so the same bytes cannot return.
this.sql.exec('CREATE TABLE IF NOT EXISTS removed (room TEXT NOT NULL, id TEXT NOT NULL, PRIMARY KEY(room, id)) STRICT');
  • objects_v2: ciphertext with its room key, hash, padded size, expiry and insertion sequence.
  • reads: one digest per accepted read until its window closes.
  • removed: identifiers the operator removed.
  • relay_schema: the schema version (2). An unknown version stops the object rather than serving data.
  • objects: version 1 rows from an upgraded relay, never served, counted against the service quota and deleted at their expiry.

It holds no writer keys, grants, community keys, MLS headers, content keys, names, profiles, member lists or plaintext posts, and no request headers: a test uploads with X-Forwarded-For and a cookie and checks neither is stored. The relay cannot prove a payload is encrypted; clients must encrypt before uploading. Application code does not log requests, addresses or bodies, and the checked-in config turns Worker observability off; the hosting provider still processes connection metadata.

Each setting must be an integer from 1 to its bound, or the object refuses to start. Storage limits can be lowered, never raised past these bounds; the object count limit exists because the byte limit alone would allow too many tiny objects and signatures. Quota checks and inserts run in one transaction, so competing uploads cannot overshoot, and legacy v1 bytes count toward the service totals. Rate counters are global fixed one-minute windows in memory, keep no requester identifiers and reset when the object restarts: they bound work but give no per-person fairness. Admission is possession of an epoch's room key, not proof of a distinct person.

The relay deletes objects at their expiry by a Durable Object alarm set to the earliest expiry, or when a request touches them first; read digests go by alarm shortly after their window. Holders drop pieces past their expiry day when they sweep, and a full holder first sheds expired pieces, then pieces nobody asked for in 30 days (Scattered delivery). The core keeps a copy of everything it sent until it is acknowledged; an object that expired unsent is dropped from the outbox and everything is sent again.

lanes: Member | Relay | Alarm Member => Relay: PUT, X-Hoi-Expires = now + 30 days Relay: stores it; sets the alarm to the earliest expiry Member -> Relay: listings and fetches, each signed once Relay: each read's digest kept 300 s Alarm -> Relay: at the digest's expiry: delete read digests Alarm -> Relay: at the object's expiry: delete it, schedule the next Member -> Relay: GET the object after it expired Relay --> Member: 404 not-found Member: upload again only with a fresh signed envelope

Operator controls

There is no HTTP endpoint for deletion. An operator who wants identified objects or rooms gone (a report that names a room, or a room and an object) stores the list as the Worker secret HOI_RELAY_PURGE with the local tool, which runs Wrangler:

node relay/operator.mjs remove --dry-run <room>/<id> <room>
node relay/operator.mjs remove --check https://relay.example/relay <room>/<id> <room>
  • Entries: <room> (every object of the room, and no uploads to it afterwards), <room>/<id> (one object, which cannot be stored again) or v1 (every legacy object), separated by commas or whitespace, at most 4 KiB per batch (secrets hold at most 5 KiB). The tool validates entries before calling Wrangler; an invalid list stops the relay with 503 rather than serve what it was told to remove.
  • Applying: setting the secret deploys a new Worker version; the Durable Object applies the list on its next start, in one transaction with the schema check, records each identifier in removed and deletes matching rows. The deletion runs again at every start, so storage restored from an earlier point is purged again. Each run replaces the secret; earlier batches stay in removed.
  • Checking: --check takes the HTTPS relay base (or a loopback one), wakes the object through /relay/v2/meta and prints the removed count before and after, retrying for up to a minute. --dry-run prints the Wrangler command and changes nothing.
  • Who learns what: members of a removed room get 410 removed after a valid room signature; nobody else learns whether a room existed or was removed. The meta count is public, like a transparency report; the identifiers are not. Removal does not touch members' devices and cannot identify who uploaded an object.

For a local Worker, the same list goes in with wrangler dev --var HOI_RELAY_PURGE:<list>.

Running the relay

The relay is a Cloudflare Worker of its own; the static website contains none. The Worker routes only /relay/… to the Durable Object and answers 404 for anything else.

# Standalone encrypted storage; independent operators use their own Worker/account.
name = "hoi-opaque-relay"
main = "worker.mjs"
compatibility_date = "2026-10-01"
compatibility_flags = ["nodejs_compat"]

[observability]
enabled = false

[[durable_objects.bindings]]
name = "OPAQUE_RELAY"
class_name = "OpaqueRelay"

[[migrations]]
tag = "opaque-relay-v1"
new_sqlite_classes = ["OpaqueRelay"]
pnpm dlx wrangler@4 deploy --config relay/wrangler.toml
pnpm dlx wrangler@4 deploy --config relay/wrangler.toml --dry-run
pnpm dlx wrangler@4 dev --config relay/wrangler.toml --local
  • The migration creates the SQLite class; no database identifier or decryption secret is provisioned. Keep the Worker name, class name, binding and migration history stable to keep the stored objects.
  • HOI_RELAY_ORIGINS: comma-separated exact app origins for an externally hosted app or native shell, for example https://app.example,http://tauri.localhost,tauri://localhost. Each must be a bare http(s) origin, or tauri://localhost. The request URL's own origin is always allowed.
  • The app is then built with that relay's base URL in its relay list (the setting is named in apps/web/README.md), at most three bases.
  • It is a bounded pilot, not an unlimited archive or a consensus network. Several instances run by one operator are not independent replicas; another operator deploys it in their own account under their own URL. Hosting objects grants no voting or signing authority.
Upgrading from version 1

Version 1 is not served. A relay upgraded from it keeps v1 rows unreadable until they expire (31 days at most), or until the operator removes v1. Clients on version 2 send live records and media again into the current room; a membership change that was still waiting for a v1 relay is lost, so the devices it concerned are removed and invited again.

Trust boundary

An operator can withhold, delete or replay stored ciphertext and can inspect the metadata above; a mailbox holder can do the same with its slots. Clients therefore validate downloaded hashes and authenticated content themselves and never treat a listing, its order or a receipt as permission to accept a message: the core decides validity, and a commit is followed only from a committer, in order. Independent relays reduce dependence on one host; they do not solve malicious membership, collusion or the authenticity of a newly downloaded app. The relay is not a membership authority.
