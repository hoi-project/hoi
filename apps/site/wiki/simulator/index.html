The simulator

hoi-sim attacks the protocol and reports whether it held. It reimplements no rule: honest participants are real hoi-core nodes driven through the ordinary API, hostile ones send raw wire bytes over the sync protocol, and the models of scattered delivery and the ladder call the same functions every device runs. So every number it prints is the protocol's own verdict.

hoi-sim with no command, help, -h or --help prints the command list and the forge attacks. An unknown command or option prints an error and exits with failure. It is a developer tool: it depends on hoi-core like any other host and ships in no release. The crate's dependencies are on Crates.

battle: the in-process siege

hoi-sim battle [--honest N] [--attackers M] [--days D] [--pow-bits B] [--json]
--honestdefault 4, at least 2
Honest members besides the founder.
--attackersdefault 3, at least 1
Forgers, each also running a spam flood.
--daysdefault 30
Only printed in the scorecard's header; the siege advances its own clock.
--pow-bitsdefault 8
The community's stamp difficulty. Cheap but real stamps, so a siege runs in seconds.
--jsonflag
Print the scorecard as JSON instead of the scoreboard.

The setup

Everything runs on Ephemeral storage under one shared clock starting at 1_790_000_000. The hub is a headless node (platform node) serving at ws://127.0.0.1:7842, like a session's hoi-node; the topology is a star. The founder mira creates the community privacy with fast limits: stamp difficulty from --pow-bits, 30-day retention, four newcomer posts a day, identity work 4 bits, one established voucher to admit, established after one day, a one-hour contest window. Admission on a single vouch is deliberate: the Sybil ring gets in on the turncoat's one vouch, so what the ladder does with it shows clearly. Every honest node joins with the invite and pairs with the hub through a fresh pairing link, since each link pairs one device.

Honest actions go through the API (create_post, vouch, review, set_limits); after each step the battle delivers every queued message in both directions until the star goes quiet (at most 200 passes) and commits every node. Attackers are Forgers with no node at all: they connect to the hub as a Client, send a hello with the hub's current pairing token, then recs in chunks of 500, stopping if the hub cuts them off.

The phases and the invariants

  1. Honest members join

    The founder vouches every honest member in; the clock moves a day and a minute. Check: “honest members become established by being vouched in and waiting”.

  2. They post

    One friendly post each (honest_posts).

  3. Attack 1: a forgery barrage

    Every attacker sends every refusable artifact of the whole catalogue, without introducing its identity first: a key with no identity work is one of the things that must bounce. Check: “forged records (bad signatures, backdating, oversize, unstamped, no identity, …) never reach the feed”, counted by author in the founder's feed.

  4. Attack 2: a spam flood

    Each attacker brings a fresh key with valid credentials and five CHEAP PILLS posts. Every honest member then reviews each visible spam post as hide / spam. Check: “spam is held for newcomers and the community naps the rest without a moderator”: none left showing un-napped.

  5. Attack 3: a Sybil ring

    Six fresh keys are introduced; the established member h0 turns and vouches all six in, in one quarter. A day later: Check: “a Sybil ring vouched in together shares one voucher's voice, never multiplies it”: the ring's voice summed is at most the turncoat's.

  6. Attack 4: the mob

    Every Sybil hides the last honest member, objects to them (a −1 vouch, spam) and hides each of their posts as off-topic. Check: “a Sybil mob cannot block a real member (voice, not heads, decides)”.

  7. Governance

    The founder proposes raising identity_bits to 16, which the honest electors never agree to. Check: “rules and limits change only by the community's consent, not an owner alone”: the limit is unchanged.

  8. Churn, when asked for

    With churn, a different share of honest members is offline at each settling: they drop their link and pair again when back. At the end everyone returns. Check: “members who came and went catch up with everything once back”.

  9. Convergence

    Check: “honest nodes holding the same records compute the same standing”: every honest node agrees with the founder's node on every member's level.

The command runs siege, which is siege_with_churn with no churn: seven checks. The tests also run it with churn (below), which adds the eighth.

The scorecard

report.rs keeps one Check { invariant, held, detail } per invariant and free-form tallies: honest_posts, forged_injected, spam_posted, spam_reached_hub, sybil_ring_size, and churn_percent with churn. The text form:


  hoi protocol siege · scenario: siege · 4 honest, 3 adversarial · 30 days simulated
  ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  ✓ held   honest members become established by being vouched in and waiting
            4/4 honest members are established
  ✓ held   forged records (bad signatures, backdating, oversize, unstamped, no identity, …) never reach the feed
            feed held at <posts> legit posts; 39 malformed records injected, 0 displayed
  ✓ held   spam is held for newcomers and the community naps the rest without a moderator
            15 spam posts, 0 left showing after community hides
  ✓ held   a Sybil ring vouched in together shares one voucher's voice, never multiplies it …
            6 sybils hold <x> voice between them; their voucher holds <y> — the ring did not multiply it
  …
  ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  counters: honest_posts=…  forged_injected=39  spam_posted=15  spam_reached_hub=…  sybil_ring_size=6
  ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  VERDICT: the protocol held. 7/7 invariants stood against the attack.

A broken check prints ✗ BROKE and the verdict “the protocol BROKE. n/m invariants held; see ✗ above.”. Each attacker injects 13 refusable forgeries (every attack gives one, dup-flood five), so three attackers make 39; five spam posts each make 15. With --json the same Scorecard is printed as {scenario, honest, attackers, days, checks: [{invariant, held, detail}], tallies: [[name, n]]}.

Forged attacks

A Forger is what a real attacker's tool would be: a root key with the cheapest acceptable identity work (MIN_BITS, 4 bits) and a device key it blessed, able to sign anything, well formed or not. credentials(now) gives its identity and delegation records, each stamped to the global floor of 16 bits. forge(attack, forger, community, now, bits) builds the artifacts, stamped to the community's pow_bits so each one gets past the stamp and reaches the stage it tests.

AttackWhat it buildsExpectedWhat must refuse it
bad-sigA stamped post whose signature has its first byte flippedrefusedthe signature check
futureA post dated an hour aheadrefusedthe time check: created_at beyond now plus the 600-second clock skew on live gossip
expiredA post made two hours ago that expired an hour agorefusedthe time check
unstampedA signed post with nonce 0refusedthe stamp floor, for the price of one hash
oversizeA post with 70,000 characters of textrefuseddecoding: past the 64 KiB cap for posts
noncanonicalA valid wire with one trailing 0x00refusedthe strict decoder
dup-floodSix posts with the same copy-pasted text, a second apartfirst contained, five refusedthe duplicate-text fingerprint within 7 days and the rate windows
no-identityA post from a device whose root never published an identityrefusedthe identity check
wrong-root-delegationA delegation of someone else's device key, blessed by the attacker's root but signed by the attacker's own devicerefusedconsent: a delegation must be signed by the device it delegates

"Contained" means a correct node may accept the record but must hold it or count it late: it must never reach the feed. The module also has helpers the strategies use: review (a hide, veto or ok signed with the 8-bit kind discount), backdated_vouch (a vouch claiming an earlier time, which a node that had caught up counts from arrival), hostile_ledger (a ledger body for governance capture) and fake_media_ref.

agent: one participant against a hub

hoi-sim agent --data DIR [--hub URL] [--name NAME] [--days D] [--pow-bits B] <verb> [args]

An agent keeps its identity and records in DIR/hoi.db (plain SQLite, platform desktop, identity work at the default 10 bits), so each --data directory is one independent member and an agent can be run as many times, from as many sessions, as you like. The hub is --hub ws://…, or the home node the directory paired with. Every result is pretty-printed JSON; failures are {"ok": false, "error": "…"} with exit status 1. This is what the Docker sandbox drives.

hoi-sim agent --data ./mira  --name mira  init
hoi-sim agent --data ./mira  pair 'hoi://pair?...'      # the hub's pairing link
hoi-sim agent --data ./mira  --hub ws://hub:7842 create privacy
hoi-sim agent --data ./mallory --name mallory init
hoi-sim agent --data ./mallory pair 'hoi://pair?...'
hoi-sim agent --data ./mallory --hub ws://hub:7842 join 'hoi://join?...'
hoi-sim agent --data ./mallory --hub ws://hub:7842 post <community-hex> "title" "text"
hoi-sim agent --data ./mallory --hub ws://hub:7842 vouch <community-hex> <key>

create's sandbox limits: stamp difficulty --pow-bits or 12, retention 7 days, four newcomer posts a day, identity work 8 bits, one voucher to admit, a ten-minute contest window, and established after --days × 60 seconds (30 minutes by default; 300 seconds with --days 0), so a pentest doesn't wait two weeks. Its rules are “1. Be kind. 2. No spam. 3. Stay on topic.”

One session

Every verb but init, pair, join, create, invite and link-approve needs an identity and a hub, and runs inside wire::session: a one-shot WebSocket client that mirrors hoi_net::dial without looping forever.

lanes: Agent node | wire.rs | Hub wire.rs -> Hub: WebSocket connect (message cap MAX_MESSAGE) Agent node: net_connect("hub", Role::Home) Agent node => Hub @sky: hello, catch-up, records, until quiet Hub => Agent node @sky: what the hub has wire.rs: quiet for 400 ms, or 20 s in all Agent node: act(node): the verb's one action Agent node => Hub @sky: what the action produced, until quiet again wire.rs -> Hub: close Agent node: net_disconnect("hub"), commit()

forge, with a local oracle

agent … forge <attack> <community> builds the attack with a fresh Forger at the community's pow_bits (8 if unknown). First it feeds each artifact to the agent's own node with ingest, as if a peer named oracle sent it, and records the exact stage that refused it. Then wire::inject connects, syncs, pushes the forger's credentials and the artifacts as raw recs in chunks of 500, syncs again and reports whether the hub kept the link (it drops peers that send too many invalid records).

{
  "ok": true,
  "attack": "bad-sig",
  "community": "…",
  "injected_to_hub": true,
  "hub_kept_link": true,
  "all_held_locally": true,
  "forgeries": [
    { "forgery": "post with a forged signature", "expected": "refused",
      "local_verdict": "refused at: …", "held": true, "note": "signature doesn't verify over the id" }
  ],
  "reading": "every forgery should be refused or contained; run the referee to confirm the honest network never shows it"
}

The exit status is 1 when any artifact was not held locally. Whether the honest network shows any of it is the referee's question.

referee: the scoreboard

hoi-sim referee --data DIR [--hub URL] [--community HEX | -c HEX] [--watch] [--json]

An honest observer. Without an identity it makes a quiet one (named --name or referee). It syncs in a session and reads, for every joined community (or only --community): name and id, established members, moderators, bans, pending proposals, posts showing and napping in the fresh feed, and, for everyone who posted, their short key, level, voice to three decimals, and whether they are blocked or struck.

With --watch it clears the screen and prints again every five seconds until interrupted (one compact JSON line per round with --json); without it, once. The JSON is {ok, hub, communities: [{id, name, members, mods, banned, proposals, posts_visible, posts_napping, people: [{key, level, voice, blocked, struck}]}]}.

ladder: hundreds of members, adversarial vouching

hoi-sim ladder [--days D] [--seed S]        # defaults: 360 days, seed 1

A model, with no nodes and no network: the community's records so far go through hoi_moderation::ladder::settle, the function every device runs, once a week. Quarters line up with the ladder's 90-day vouch epoch. The community uses the default ladder with two established vouchers to admit and 14 days to become established.

  • Honest life. About one newcomer a day (90%) until the community is full, vouched in by up to two established honest members who are around. Members post (30% a day), go away for 20 to 79 days now and then, a few leave for good; established members come across recent posts (20% a day), hide spam and defend members they vouched for.
  • Governance. The owner proposes a change on day 15 of every 30; honest electors agree within the week, or leave it unanswered.
StrategyThe party plays
noneNo party: the baseline.
ringSybils vouch only for each other. Nobody established ever vouched for them.
turncoatFrom day 60, two established members turn together (one alone can't admit anyone with two vouchers needed). Each quarter each vouches a batch of four Sybils in, the other countersigning, and every established Sybil does the same. The ring hides honest posts, tries to block an honest member, objects to every proposal and posts spam.
patientSybils that behave: each talks an honest member into a vouch now and then (15% a quarter), they vouch for each other, post well and wait; from day 180 all strike at once.

The report runs every strategy at 100 and at 300 honest members, one line each, under the header hoi-sim ladder · <days> days · settled weekly with hoi_moderation::ladder::settle · seed <seed>:

ColumnMeaning
admitted(h/p)Honest people and party members who reached member or above.
share max/endThe party's share of established voice: the largest seen at a weekly settle, and at the end.
quarters over 2×Quarters in which the party's voice grew past twice what it had (or one voice), plus what honest vouches handed it: the bound the ladder is built on. Must be 0.
blocked(h/p)Honest people and party members the community blocked.
hidden honest, spam hiddenHonest posts hidden; spam posts hidden out of those posted.
proposals passed/lapsed/openThe owner's proposals.
late electorsElectors of a proposal vouched in after it was made. Must stay 0.

The tests (200 days, 80 members, seed 7) pin the results down: a ring nobody vouched for is admitted nowhere and holds no voice; a turncoat ring gets in but never more than doubles a quarter, blocks nobody honest, and adds no late electors; patient Sybils stay within what they were given; without a party at least five proposals are made and none lapses.

scatter: placement against a party

hoi-sim scatter [--trials N] [--seed S]     # defaults: 20,000 trials, seed 1

Generations of pieces placed with the core's own rules (hoi_sync::place::place) among places a party runs a share of, sized with the core's own function (hoi_crypto::scatter::size::size). Each place is a node in a network of its own, so a fair party's draws are exactly hypergeometric. Each trial spends a generation's whole index budget, the worst case repair can reach: n pieces first, then m − n more on places that never held a sibling. It counts whether the party ended up able to rebuild (k of all m placed) or to withhold (more than n − k of the n live).

The report covers 30, 80, 100, 400 and 1,000 places, levels 10, 15 and 20, and party shares of 5, 10, 15, 20 and 30%, one line each:

places  level  party   k/n (m)    rebuilt (exact)            withheld (exact)           most  hard
  1000    10%    10%   k/n   (  m)       0 ( 1.234e-3 )        0 ( 5.678e-4 )   …
k/n (m)Size
The size the core chose for generation 0 at that level and number of places.
rebuilt (exact)count (expected)
Trials where the party could rebuild, and the exact chance times the trials, from hyper_tail(places, party, m, k).
withheld (exact)count (expected)
The same for withholding, hyper_tail(places, party, n, n − k + 1).
mostpieces
The most pieces of one generation the party ever held.
hardyes or blank
The party runs fewer places than k: it can never rebuild, whatever the luck.

Two closing lines show the poisoned case at 400 and 1,000 places: half the places are the party's, all reached through one introducer (a poisoned node list), which placement caps at ⌈n/4⌉ of a generation. The tests check that the floating-point tail agrees with known values (15 of 28 with a budget of 35 at 10% in a large network ≈ 4.6·10⁻⁷), that a tenth of the places in networks of 12 to 90 never rebuilds or withholds, that observed counts stay within five standard deviations of the formula far above the level, that at the level's own share nothing happens in 3,000 trials, and that a poisoned node list or poisoned rosters get no more than ⌈n/4⌉ and rebuild nothing, where the same places reached fairly rebuild in more than half the trials.

scatter --life: objects over their whole life

hoi-sim scatter --life [--objects N] [--days D] [--seed S]   # defaults: 1,000 objects, 90 days

A network of places that come and go. Objects are placed and sized with the core's functions and repaired the way members' durable devices repair them, four rounds a day (every six hours):

  • An index is lost when its holder answered "not kept", or stayed silent for more than two probes in a row (half a day): a holder only briefly away is not lost. Honest holders answer 90% of probes.
  • Repair starts once a third of the spare pieces are gone (size::repair_margin): fresh indexes go to places that never held one of the generation, within the budget; with the budget spent, the next generation, sized for its smaller share of the risk, at most once a day unless fewer than k are left. Readers keep the generation before as a fallback.
  • Every placement is carried by three forwarder places, at most k − 1 indexes of a generation each; the most any carried is recorded.
  • Places leave for good at the churn rate each day and a new node takes the slot; the party keeps its share.

The party keeps every piece it is ever given. It may force repairs (its holders answer "not kept" for everything, so devices re-place those indexes elsewhere and the party gets more draws), and a member of it may make bad updates (a new generation onto its own places once a day, which it then withholds).

The tests hold it to at least 99.9% readable at 10% churn a day in networks of 80 and 1,000 places with nothing lost, rebuilt or withheld; forced repairs buying the party no more than the exact tails allow; and bad updates delaying a post without making it unreadable.

scatter --load: a peak hour

hoi-sim scatter --load [--seed S]

One peak hour of a network with 10,000 to 200,000 devices online, sampled. There is one public node per hundred devices online (at least 12), each a holder and a forwarder in a place of its own, linked to 64 others and vouching for 32 (or 64) of them. 400 sampled devices keep three forwarder links (70%, browsers and phones) or six (30%, desktops); a device reaches the holders its forwarders vouch for and places a generation among them; a reader can rebuild only if k of the holders are among those its own forwarders reach.

scatter --address: who can link an address to a record

hoi-sim scatter --address [--trials N] [--seed S]

A device sends through forwarders it links to; a party runs a share of the public nodes. Two things could tie the device's address to what it does: its own records (a post's pointer, a vote, a reply) and its uploads of pieces. The model compares two designs for parties running 5, 10 and 20% of the nodes. In the row labeled before, the device offers each new record to its own links, so the first link has both, and uploads go in two hops. In the row labeled since S7, the current code, a record goes sealed through an entry link to an injector, another of its links, and uploads go in three hops: linking needs two of the party's nodes on one path. Columns: the share of own records one node links by itself, the share the party links with all its nodes, and the same two for uploads.

The test checks that before, the first link always could (1.0), that now no single node can (0.0), and that the party's share is about the square of its share of the nodes, within 0.01 over 20,000 trials.
